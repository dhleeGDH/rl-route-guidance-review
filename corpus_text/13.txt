ABSTRACT

ARTICLE HISTORY

Travelers always perform some preference during the decision-making process. The preference will affect
the decision results and can be improved by continuously learning. In order to understand the influence of
individual preference on travel behavior choice , two individual preferences, including indifference pre­
ference and compulsive preference are considered in the paper. Two updating mechanisms of compulsive
preference are proposed to obtain the choosing probability of all alternatives. Reinforcement learning
models are established integrating the gain stimulating and loss stimulating considering expected utility.
Nguyen Dupuis network is adopted for numerical simulation to study the updating process. Simulation
results denote that the equilibrium state is much more efficient when preference learning mechanism is
considered comparing with the traditional stochastic user equilibrium model, and can decrease the total
travel time greatly, which can be applied for urban traffic management. Personalized traffic guidance is the
effective solution to traffic congestion in the future

Introduction
Route choice modeling is the fundamental and critical component
in transportation systems analysis and planning. Generally, factors
that influencing travelers’ route choice behavior includes travel
attributes, road preference, and socio-economic attributes (Sun
et al. 2014). Various behavior mechanisms of route choice have
been proposed (Mahmassani and Chang 1987; Sun, Cheng, and Ma
2018; Zhang and Yang 2015), and equilibrium assignment is the
traditional and classic model which is based on utility maximization
theory (Beckmann, Mcguire, and Winster 1956).
However, utility maximization mechanism has been criticized
for ignoring the difference of travelers’ individual preference and
cognition (Simon 1972). Then, bounded rationality has been widely
adopted for behavior analysis (Mahmassani and Chang 1987).
Numerous practical cases show that travelers’ route choice behavior
exhibits significant bounded rationality, which means travelers may
not necessarily choose the route with the minimum cost (Li et al.
2019).
Bounded rationality considering travelers’ limit capability of
finding the best alternative, which is due to travelers’ individual
preferences. The subjective intentions, reference point, individual
habits, the attitudes toward risk, and the learning abilities all can
summarized as the preference. Travelers’ preferences may change
depending on various factors, such as the travel purpose, the
departure time, the travel mode, and travel time. Due to the differ­
ent preferences, travelers’ understanding and requirements for the
optimal path may also vary from person to person. Therefore, the
route choice behavior may not entirely the same for all drivers.
Traffic assignment is always applied to study the route choice
model. Table 1 lists the characteristic of some commonly adopted
models.
The traditional traffic assignment models do not consider tra­
velers’ preferences, which assume that all travelers are uniformly
fully rational. The current study about subjective preferences most
focuses on static travel behavior and cannot describe travelers’

The article establishes a reinforced learning (RL) model incor­
porating two kinds of preferences, describes the day-to-day learning
process of route choice. The learning model considers both indif­
ference band and compulsive preference. Indifference band denotes
the similar perceptive utility for the two different routes, and
compulsive preference denotes the loyalty and exclusion of some
alternatives. The two kinds of preference comprehensively describe
travelers’ psychological states when choosing paths.
Regret has been introduced into the dynamic reinforcement
learning model, and the stimulation has been generated by all the
historical experience, not only the current trip. The learning model
establishes the relationship between psychological preferences and
route choice behavior by introducing perceived stimulation, which
explains the influence of travelers’ preferences on route choice
behavior at each trip considering the regret or joyful when they
are faced with loss or gain. The reinforcement learning model
reveals the mechanism of learning, strengthening, and feedback of
subjective preferences.

Literature review
Travelers’ preference
Preference involved in bounded rationality includes mispercep­
tions, prevailing choice set, satisfactory principle and choice pre­
ference, and so on, which always exhibits as choosing the same
alternatives repeatedly, sticking to or excluding some fixed choices
(Di et al. 2017). Preference is mostly dominated by the individual’s
habit strength, sensitivity of gain and loss, and difference of refer­
ence point.
Indifference band is generally used for quantifying the preference
in the current research, which is a well-known and ubiquitous
mechanism derived from the perspective of bounded rationality.
The consideration means that, if the travel cost difference between
current choice and the best alternatives is less than the indifference
band, travelers will not change his/her choice and stick to the current
strategy. Indifference band has been widely applied in discrete mod­
els of route choice (Diana and Ceccato 2019; Sun, Cheng, and Ma
2018; Yu, Han, and Ochieng 2020) and traffic equilibrium models
(David et al. 2018; Guo, Wang, and Wang 2022).
There are several estimation methods have been proposed to
determine the width of indifference band. The quantification of
indifference band varied: 10 min (Mahmassani and Chang 1985),
5–10 min, or 17%–22% (Srinivasan and Mahmassani 1999), and
5%–20% (Di et al. 2017). Some researchers discovered that indif­
ference band existed due to switching inertia and flow patterns (van
Essen et al. 2015; Zhang and Yang 2015). Additionally, indifference
band may be a function of average travel time, travel time differ­
ence, and travelers’ individual characteristics (Carrion and
Levinson 2019; Zhu and Levinson 2012).
Preference is also widely expressed as inertia. Inertia is charac­
terized as travelers sticking to previous choice and do not necessa­
rily minimizing their travel time, which is usually considered as
a major exogenous stochastic variable. Thorhauge, Swait, and
Cherchi (2020) found that inertia was influenced by gender, pre­
sence of children in the household as well as work type, and higher
inertia levels would lead to higher rescheduling penalties and lower
willing to shift departure time. Zhang and Yang (2015) defined
three levels of inertia (those are fully non-inertial, partially inertial,
and fully inertial), incorporated inertia into the traffic assignment
as an exogenous stochastic factor, and proposed a model of inertial

Traveler’s subjective preference has been widely accepted in the
field of route choice. However, preference was usually considered as
an exogenous variable, which has been considered as specific values
through assumptions. The internal mechanism of preference related
to travel behavior has not been revealed clearly. Only one kind of
preference has been considered in current studies, such as indiffer­
ence band, inertia, and loyalty. Additionally, the inertia behavior of
excluding some fixed choices was rarely involved. The description of
travel preference cannot reflect the authentic travel behavior.
Reinforced learning (RL)
Travelers’ preference may be strengthened or improved by RL
according to the current information and previous experience
(Busemeyer and Townsend 1993; Erev and Barron 2005). RL is
the dominant method to describe behavior under uncertainty,
which is usually described by statistical models.
Reinforced learning model is used to describe the day-to-day
route choice behavior. Zhang et al. (2018) studied the interactive
learning mechanism among travelers considering friends’ travel
information. Qin et al. (2019) established a stochastic, dynamic
model to reproduce the repeated decision-making process based
on decision field theory. Zhou et al. (2020) introduced the Bush–
Mosteller (B-M) RL scheme to model the route choice behaviors of
the travelers. The above researches focused on how behavior learn­
ing affects the equilibrium flow. Meanwhile, RL was also adopted
on travel mode choice in a long time (Marek 2018).
Additionally, some other learning modes have been proposed
and applied to travel behavior study. Ma and Pace (2017) estab­
lished a four-strategy learning model, including reinforcement
learning, extended reinforcement learning, joint strategy fictitious
play , and Bayesian learning. Xiong, Zhou, and Zhang (2018)
established a new learning model considering the attributes of
paths and modes. Yu and Gao (2019) proposed two kinds of routing
policy, including one-step prediction (day-to-day) and full-

The current learning model only considers objective gains and
losses during the evolution process, without reflecting travelers’
subjective preferences. At the same time, fixed values has been
applied as the indicators of measuring gains and losses. However,
the reference point (that is the expected returns) will change with
the learning process. As elaborated above, little attention has been
paid on how do the expected utility, previous experience, learning
ability affect traveler’s day-to-day route choice behavior. Then RL
mechanism cannot be clearly revealed because of the one-sidedness
of individual preference.
Travelers’ route choice mechanism is the premise of route gui­
dance. However, the current traffic guidance strategies consider the
travelers are completely rational and the same guidance alternatives
have been recommended to different travelers, which cannot
achieve the goal of resource’s optimal allocation.
Overall, the article introduces two kinds of subjective prefer­
ences, namely the indifference preference and compulsive prefer­
ence, to construct a route choice model. Updating the route choice
probability through exclusivity and loyalty preferences, which more
comprehensively reveal the impact of subjective preferences on
travel behavior. Integrating subjective preferences into RL model,
the article establishes revenue and loss stimulation models through
continuously updating expected returns based on historical returns
considering subjective preferences. This study establishes an inter­
nal logical relationship between objective gains/losses and subjec­
tive preferences, further revealing the inherent impact of subjective
preferences on travelers’ dynamic decision-making behavior. In
practice, the research establishes the relationship between micro
behavioral preferences and macro traffic flow, which provides
a theoretical basis for travel guidance.

Traveler’s preference
Different from previous research, two individual preferences are
discussed in this paper, including indifference preference and com­
pulsive preference. Sometimes, travelers will not switch to the short­
est route if the benefit of switching is within the indifference band,
because the optimal route has the similar perceptive utility to the old
one. On the contrary, some travelers may have a compulsive pre­
ference for one or several alternatives, including liking or disliking
extremely. If the traveler prefers a certain route particularly, the route
always has a high probability of being selected. Otherwise, if the
traveler excludes a certain alternative for some reasons, the selected
probability of the option will be much smaller than other routes.

Discussion
① Stable flow comparison. Average stable flow of each section
under different cases is shown in Figure 9.
Section’s flow under case 3 is greatly different from case 1 and
case 2. The results denote that network volume can reach
a relatively consistent stable state no matter the updating pattern
of preference, and whether considering the compulsive preference
and indifference threshold is the key factor determining the equili­
brium state.
② Convergence speed comparison. According to the previous
analysis, the convergence speed of case 3 is higher than that in case
1 and case 2, and the convergence speed in case 1 and case 2 are
similar. The results mean that, the system converges slowly when
individual preference is considered. For cases 1 and 2, different
indifference bands and smoothing coeficients will adjust the switch­
ing behavior constantly, then all the sections’ flows change greatly,
therefore much more itrations are needed for equilibrium.
③ Average total travel time comparison. The average total travel
time of the network in each case is shown in Table 5.
The total travel time for case 3 is the longest among all the
simulation scenarios. Therefore, the network can converge and
reach the equilibrium state rapidly under the SUE allocation
principle (case 3), but the unreasonable flow allocation leads to
long total travel time. When the preference is considered and
constantly updated according to the gains and losses (case 2),
we can obtain the least total travel time and highest travel
efficiency.
For the SUE model, travelers always pursue the maximum
utility, then all travelers will choose the route with minimum
travel time. Under the strict route choosing criterion, each tra­
veler has only one feasible route, then the assignment proposal is
unique. However, travelers may choose the satisfactory route not
necessarily the shortest path when travelers’ preference is con­
sidered because of the different tolerance threshold. That is, all
travelers may have several feasible satisfactory routes to choose.
Some travelers will be allocated according to the traffic state of
each section, which can more evenly distribute the traffic to all
sections.

Conclusions
The paper introduces learning stimulation and builds a route
choice reinforcement learning model considering traveler’s pre­
ferences. Two mechanisms of preference updating including
fixed preference and updating preference according to gains
or losses are proposed. The learning model introduces gain
stimulation and loss stimulation simultaneously, and updates
the preferences based on the expected utility, previous experi­
ence and forecasted information. Taking Nguyen Dupuis net­
work as an example, convergence process and travel efficiency
of the stable state corresponding to different scenarios are
simulated considering variation of preference level and indif­
ference threshold. The efficiency and adaptability of the models
are analyzed through comparing with SUE model. The conclu­
sions are concluded as follows:
(1) For case 1, flow of the sections with high preference level
fluctuates greatly during the initial iterations (scenario 3 and
scenario 4). There is no significant difference on the final
equilibrium flow under case 1 no matter the preference level.
It shows that the preference level only influences the flow
allocation at the initial iterations, and the equilibrium flow
depends on the gain and loss stimulation during the deci­
sion-making process. Additionally, some sections with
lower overlap coefficient will be chosen with higher prob­
ability because of the gain stimulation originating from
high-preference routes.
(2) The system convergence process of case 2 is gentler than
case 1, and almost no large fluctuation occurs. Therefore,
reinforcement learning model based on preference updating
can ensure the system stability.
(3) For case 3 (SUE), equilibrium flow of the sections with highoverlap coefficient decreases with the decrease of perception
error, and sections with low-overlap coefficient denote the
contrary tendency.
(4) The system has the lowest travel efficiency for case 3 com­
paring with case 1 and case 2, while allocation pattern of case
2 can achieve the highest travel efficiency. It indicates that
the reinforcement learning mode of preference updating is
helpful to improve the overall operation efficiency and
volume’ rational allocation.

The paper introduces a new behavior mechanism, that the route
probability is affected both by internal preferences and external
benefits, which further explains the route choice behavior motiva­
tion. Two kinds of preference (indifference band and compulsive
preference) are taken into account in the learning model, which
supplements the research on traveler preference. The learning
mechanism makes up for the defect that the expected utility and
past experience are not considered in the current methods.
Travelers’ heterogeneity is of great significance for optimizing
the allocation of traffic resources.The results can be adopted to
urban traffic management. For traffic managers, route guidance
algorithm should consider the heterogeneity preference and rein­
forcement learning ability of travelers, then the optimal route with
the highest operation efficiency will be recommended to travelers.
The route guidance considering the travelers’ external and internal
heterogeneity can effectively reduce the total travel time. One
person one program will be the topical subject of traffic guidance
in the future.
However, the travel preference considered in the paper is
still one-sided to some extent. Due to the different socioeco­
nomic characteristic and travel attributes, traveler’s preference
varies from time to time. Therefore, the travel preferences of
different travel groups and trip purposes should be considered
to build the reinforcement learning model of route choice,
based on the heterogeneity preference, which can more accu­
rately reveal the behavior evolution mechanism of travelers.
