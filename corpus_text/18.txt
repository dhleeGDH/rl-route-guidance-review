Dynamic Route Guidance System Based on Real-time Vehicle-Road
Collaborations with Deep Reinforcement Learning
Zhongqing Su and Congduan Li∗
School of Electronics and Communication Engineering, Sun Yat-sen University, Shenzhen, China
Shenzhen Key Laboratory of Navigation and Communication Integration, Shenzhen, China

Abstract— To address the potential traffic congestion in
urban transportation systems, an intelligent dynamic Route
Guidance System (RGS) is proposed, which calculates the road
weights with real-time traffic flow predictions using the reinforcement learning method. Additionally, a training strategy
is proposed for the Road Agents (RAs), which optimizes RA
through interaction with the traffic environment. The effectiveness of the proposed method is verified through simulation
on the Urban Mobility Simulation (SUMO) platform, and it
is shown that the proposed method performs well in complex
and changing urban traffic environments with different driver
participation rates. Therefore, it provides a feasible solution to
the congestion problem and will push forward the development
of intelligent transportation systems.
Index Terms— Reinforcement Learning, Route Guidance,
Traffic Congestion, Urban Transportation

I. INTRODUCTION
Urban traffic congestion is a problem that affects many
cities around the world. While government-led strategies,
such as Singapore’s congestion charging, can increase revenue, they may not fully meet expectations for alleviating
congestion [1]. According to the 2021 Urban Traffic Report
[2], even though congestion levels have decreased due to the
impact of COVID-19, the congestion problem is making a
comeback as the pandemic subsides. Therefore, we need to
find a more effective solution to alleviate the problem.
During this period, Intelligent Transportation Systems
(ITSs) have been extensively studied and widely applied in
many countries. At present, a large part of the research is
about ”traffic light control” and ”rerouting”. In traffic light
control-related research, applications based on reinforcement
learning have been very popular [3] [4]. We have also noticed that reinforcement learning methods have been further
applied in some studies related to vehicle navigation systems,
such as the problem of vehicle intersection turning [5].
However, there are few relevant technological applications
in the work associated with the ”rerouting” system.
In the following ”rerouting” literatures, congestion is regarded as a systematic problem. Therefore, it is necessary
to optimize the vehicle path planning strategy of RGS from
a global perspective to further alleviate congestion. RGS is
divided into two basic categories, ”static” and ”dynamic” [6].
The former is based on historical data, while the latter is
based on real-time data. Static RGS are no longer applicable
today, but some classic shortest path algorithms such as
Dijkstra [7] and A* [8] are still widely used.

Fig. 1: System architecture
Combined with dynamic information, more complex systems have also been proposed. For example, [9] uses intelligent transportation management systems to detect traffic
congestion and improve traffic efficiency using k-nearest
neighbor classifiers. At the same time, based on the idea
of route assignment, the k-shortest paths [10] has also been
revitalized in some systems. In [11], a systematic paradigm
is proposed to select a new route for vehicles from k paths,
considering random, entropy balance, and flow balance. [12],
[13], and others have further improved this aspect of the
work, resulting in a more complex and refined system, which
has been deployed in more distributed communication and
computing scenarios. [14] proposed a new system based on
decisive road weights, considering the maximization of realtime traffic information and road utilization.
While previous works have improved RGS, it still lacks
an intelligent road weight prediction method that combines
real-time traffic information to enable vehicles to calculate
updated routes and avoid congestion. In this paper, we
propose a dynamic road weight calculation method based
on Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and introduce
multidimensional observation information from vehicle-road
collaboration to design a more effective dynamic RGS (see
Figure 1). Our main contributions in this work are as follows:
(1) We propose the design process of DRL road agent,
including its states, actions, and rewards. (2) We propose
a training method for RGS, with the goal of using the experience gained from observations of all RAs to train a neural
network model that is generalizable to the road network.
(3) We made improvements to RGS, such as enhancing the
vehicle rerouting selection and streamlining the process of
recalculating a new path, reducing unnecessary computation
and route changes.

B. DRL Process
Unlike the common RL-based traffic lights and vehicles,
the impact of the actions of an RA on the environment
is indirect, which makes us to design alternative training
methods. We assume that there are some common characteristics among the roads in the network, and they will lead
to similar actions in response to changes in the state. For
example, congested roads are still congested in the next time
slice. Based on this shared common characteristic, this paper
proposes to train a road agent using experiences collected
from all roads, thus achieving a generalizable approach.
C. Rerouting Policy
In this subsection, we introduce our approach and explain
how to combine it with RA. Our approach improves previous
rerouting policies in two main ways. Firstly, RW calculation
is closely related to RA’s action, enabling more adaptive
prediction of road conditions for the next interval. Secondly,
we remove routes with obvious congestion conditions in
advance, which is often highly advantageous for individual
vehicles.
The proposed dynamic RGS initializes the parameters
in Table II and starts the rerouting procedure (line 2).
Then, at each time interval, the system updates real-time
traffic information, where the system detects congested road
segments, as detailed in Section II. With the help of RA,
the decisive weight for each road combined with actions is
updated. If congestion occurs, the system selects vehicles for
rerouting according to the method described in Section II.
For each rerouted vehicle, the k shortest paths are calculated
using road weights, and the average density of each path is
calculated by taking the average of the densities of the first
L road segments of the path. We remove some particularly
congested routes, where δ is consistent with the previous definition, which fully considers the current traffic conditions.
Then, we select the least popular path from the remaining
paths (which may be k −1 or k paths), and finally update the

IV. E VALUATION
A. Simulation Setup
We constructed our simulated traffic system using SUMO
1.11.0 [20] and TraCI [21]. Based on TraCI, we implemented
the proposed dynamic RGS and tested its performance using
the Selfless Traffic Routing (STR) model [22]. Essentially,
SUMO is started with TraCI options, the scene is loaded,
and then it waits for commands. This allows for real-time
operations, such as assigning new routes to specific vehicles.
Our goal is to optimize the average driving time of
vehicles, which refers to the average time for all vehicles
to travel from the source to the destination. To verify the
effectiveness of the proposed algorithm, we use two city
maps and convert them from OpenStreetMap [23] to NETCONVERT using the SUMO, as shown in Figure 3. We use
randomT rips.py provided by SUMO to generate a portion
of the vehicles that are not controlled by the system, while
using the vehicle generation function of STR to obtain the
vehicles controlled by the system. The ratio between them is
called the participation rate. Experiments are conducted with
different traffic volumes set on each map, and the proposed
DWRAkSP algorithm is used to control the rerouting of
participating vehicles. To demonstrate the performance of
the proposed method, we compare it to SP and DWkSP in
dynamic RGS.

Table II contains detailed information on two city maps.
Map1 has more similar road attributes, which may be more
conducive to the training of RA. Map2, on the other hand,
is more complex than Map1, including some expressways,
which undoubtedly poses greater challenges for the learning
of intelligent agents.
We trained our agents in the two maps, and Figure 4 shows
the convergence of the proposed algorithm. Each map had
200 simulated vehicles, assuming a participation rate of 1
and maximum learning step of 70,000. We can observe the
changes in ATT during the training process, and the ATT
decreases as the RA converges in both maps. This validates
the feasibility of the proposed algorithm.
We assume a fixed number of 200 participating vehicles
with participation rates of 1/3 and 1/4. To adapt to the more
complex environment and achieve better performance, we
set the maximum learning steps to 200,000 and 400,000
for Map1, and 100,000 and 300,000 for Map2. Despite the
more pronounced training fluctuations under low participation rates and in more complex environments, RA can still
converge in the end, as shown in Figure 5. These results
further validate the effectiveness of our method.

V. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we propose a dynamic RGS based on DRL.
By combining DRL algorithms with route guidance, the
proposed system can provide more adaptive predictions of
road conditions. Experimental results show that the system
has significant advantages in reducing average travel time
and alleviating traffic congestion. This algorithm also has
good performance in complex environments.
