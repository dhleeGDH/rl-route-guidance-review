Abstract — This paper presents a Deep Q-Learning (DQL)
approach to enhance traffic rerouting in urban vehicular
networks. The objective is to develop a dynamic route planning
system that adapts to real-time traffic conditions using AI-based
decision-making. Traffic congestion in Malaysia cities highlights
the limitations of static route planning that lacks responsiveness
to fluctuating travel demand and localized congestion. This
study addresses the need for intelligent rerouting by
manipulating learning-based models while controlling key
traffic variables such as speed, acceleration, vehicle position,
and travel time. The aim is to develop and evaluate an intelligent
rerouting system that is capable of minimizing traffic delays and
improving vehicular flow efficiency. This is achieved by
integrating Simulation of Urban Mobility (SUMO) with
MATLAB via Python Traffic Control Interface (TraCI) and
User Datagram Protocol (UDP) sockets that enable real-time
monitoring and data exchange. A deep Q-Learning agent was
trained to make rerouting decisions based on live vehicle state
inputs and traffic conditions, then the agent’s reward function
was designed to reduce congestion. Quantitative evaluations
show that DQL-based rerouting achieves 22% reduction in
average travel time compared to conventional shortest-path
routing. The proposed method demonstrates the potential of
Deep Reinforcement Learning to solve complex travel problems.
This work contributes toward intelligent transportation systems
in smart cities which enable adaptive and scalable traffic control
framework.

Conventional routing systems are limited in handling realtime parameters such as vehicle speed, acceleration, position,
and travel time which are essential factors for dynamic traffic
rerouting [14]. At the same time the systems also failed to
account for local contextual factors of Malaysia’s road
geometries and driving behaviors [10]. Most existing
solutions still depend on rigid algorithms like Dijkstra’s
method [13] or heuristic approaches [11] that cannot adapt to
changing traffic conditions. This technological gap becomes
even more critical with the emergence of autonomous vehicles
[26] and their potential to transform mixed traffic
environments [9]. The absence of localized modeling features
limits the ability to accurately evaluate vehicles interactions
under Malaysian road conditions.
This study develops and evaluates a Deep Q-Learning
(DQL) model for dynamic traffic rerouting in a simulated
Malaysian urban network that address the critical need for
adaptive solutions in the country’s congested transportation
systems. The DQL agent is trained in live vehicle parameters
which selects less congested routes to reduce average delay
and improve traffic flow. Simulations are conducted on a
SUMO modeled segment of road network based in Sandakan,
incorporating local features such as left-hand driving and
common congestion scenarios. Vehicle behavior is simulated
using Krauss and Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) of carfollowing models in a fully virtual and sensor-free
environment, aiming to test the theoretical limits of the DQL
approach under controlled experimentation, thus maintaining
its relevance to future vehicle scenarios.

Keywords — Deep Q-Learning, Traffic Route Planning,
Simulation of Urban Mobility

I. I NTRODUCTION

This paper is structured as follows: Section I presents a
review of traffic rerouting strategies. Section II outlines the
simulation framework and algorithm design. Section III
discusses the setup of the simulation environment and Deep
Q-Learning algorithm used for rerouting. Section IV presents
the experimental results with comparative analysis while
Section V concludes the findings. The structure aims to
provide a comprehensive understanding of how intelligent
rerouting is modeled, implemented, and evaluated within a
Malaysian urban traffic context. Furthermore, this
organization highlights the progressive flow from theoretical
foundations to practical implementation and validation, while
emphasizing the significance of adapting global simulation
tools to capture localized traffic dynamics for more realistic
outcomes in real-world.

Malaysia continues to face severe traffic congestion that
incurred annual productivity losses exceeding RM 15 billion,
due to high vehicle ownership which limit public
transportation use, and caused by underdeveloped
infrastructure [1]. In 2023, vehicle registration outnumbered
the population and intensified strain on urban traffic systems.
In cities like Kuala Lumpur and Kota Kinabalu, drivers spend
over 150 hours annually in congestion [3]. Traditional traffic
control systems which rely on static models and manual
interventions are struggling to adapt to the dynamic nature of
modern road networks. While global simulation like SUMO
is widely used, they are lacking built-in features to support
Malaysia -specific road behaviors such as heterogeneous
traffic flow with high percentage of motorcycles, frequent lane
discipline violations or unique junction and road designs [2].

II. REVIEW OF TRAFFIC ROUTE PLANNING

Deep Q-Learning (DQL) is built on Q-Learning by
integrating deep neural networks to generalize over large state
spaces which support real-time policy learning. As shown in
[4], DQL significantly improved route optimization and traffic
flow efficiency. [27] has implemented Dueling Deep Q
Networks (DQNs) with traffic state prediction in softwaredefined networks that achieved robustness under fluctuating
demand. [20] demonstrated that convolutional neural network
(CNN)-enhanced DQL produced more stable and accurate
decisions. Although deep reinforcement learning (DRL)
methods demand higher computational resources and
complex reward shaping, they excel in managing uncertainty
and adapting to dynamic traffic conditions, which make them
more suitable for large-scale intelligent transportation
systems.

Effective traffic route planning plays a crucial role in
reducing travel times and managing urban environments. Over
the years, routing strategies have evolved from traditional
shortest-path algorithms to intelligent AI-driven methods
capable of responding to real-time traffic conditions. This
review categorizes recent advancements into three key
groups: (1) Conventional rerouting using static algorithms,
(2) Q-Learning (QL)-based rerouting, and (3) Deep QLearning (DQL)-based rerouting. Table I summarizes the key
characteristics, benefits, and drawbacks of these approaches to
justify the selection of DQL for this study.
Conventional routing algorithms such as Dijkstra’s
algorithm [13] operates on predefined static costs and
generates shortest paths without considering real-time traffic
variation. Although they are computationally efficient, the
lack of responsiveness to sudden congestion changes makes
them unsuitable for dynamic environments. For example, [24]
demonstrated Dijkstra’s integration with Google Maps for
route optimization which proved to be efficient but inflexible.
Similarly rule-based systems reviewed in [6] and [8] revealed
limitations that make them less effective during peak hours
and under unpredictable congestion scenarios.

In essence, the reviewed literature highlights a clear
transition from static, rule-based routing methods to dynamic
and learning-based strategies. Conventional algorithms are
efficient but limited by their rigidity. Q-learning introduces
adaptability but is constrained by scalability. DQL emerges as
the most effective approach, capable of delivering real-time
and adaptive routing decisions in complex environments.
Table I reflects this trend and emphasizes the growing
consensus around DRL as the most promising direction for
future routing systems.

To overcome these limitations, researchers have explored
Q-Learning which enables agents to learn routing policies
from interaction with traffic environment. [3] have applied QLearning to improve route decisions and responsiveness while
[15] used intersection-based Q-Learning to optimize traffic
distribution and reduce delays. [12] further enhanced QLearning using Genetic Algorithms (GA) to accelerate
learning and policy exploration. However, scalability remains
a challenge due to the growing state-action space in complex
networks while [16] reported that reinforcement learning (RL)
models suffer in dense traffic conditions unless
approximations are applied.

III. M ETHODOLOGY OF TRAFFIC REROUTING

A. Traffic Rerouting Simulation Environment
The rerouting strategies evaluated in this study involve
two methods which focus on Dijkstra’s algorithm and DQL.
The Dijkstra is employed to compute the shortest path for each
vehicle before simulations begin. The algorithm uses static
edge weights based on road length or pre-assigned travel
costs. These computed routes are directly assigned to vehicle
in the SUMO route file (.rou.xml) or dynamically injected
during runtime via Python-TraCI commands such as
Vehicle.setRoute(). This method ensures each vehicle follows
the most efficient path in terms of distance but does not
account for real-time traffic conditions like congestion or
queue length. Once the simulation starts, no rerouting will
occur even if the vehicle encounters delay which makes this
method predictable but inflexible under dynamic scenario.

This section describes the proposed methodology for
implementing intelligent traffic rerouting using Deep
Q-Learning (DQL) in a simulated urban network. As shown
in Fig. 1, the system integrates SUMO microscopic traffic
simulator with Python-Traffic Control Interface (TraCI) and
MATLAB, where DQL agents are used to learn optimal route
strategies based on dynamic traffic conditions. The
architecture incorporates a feedback loop for continuous
performance evaluation and route adaptation.
The simulated platforms use SUMO, which is selected for
its open-source nature and support for real-time microscopic
traffic modeling. A real-world road network was extracted
from Java OpenStreetMap (JOSM) to maintain realistic
junction topologies, lane structures, and traffic densities.
Python-TraCI enables live vehicle monitoring and interaction
while User Datagram Protocol (UDP) communication sends
selected vehicle data to MATLAB, where route decisions are
computed and transmitted back to SUMO for execution.

B. Deep Q-Learning Algorithm for Dynamic Rerouting
This section presents the Deep Q-Learning (DQL)
algorithm developed to enhance traffic rerouting in dynamic
environments. The agent receives state input from SUMO
such as vehicle speed, delay and responds by rerouting
vehicles to minimize travel time.

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
This section presents a detailed evaluation of the proposed
Deep Q-Learning (DQL)-based routing strategy against
conventional methods. The analysis is structured across
operational, environmental, and economic dimensions that are
supported by simulations which are visualized in Fig. 3,
Fig. 4, and Table III. Each figure highlights the algorithm’s
performance improvements and discusses their significance in
the context of intelligent traffic systems. All results presented
in this paper were obtained through simulations using SUMO
and MATLAB, as SUMO is a widely recognized microscopic
traffic simulator capable of producing accurate vehicle-level
data [1].

The control logic of the system is shown in Fig. 2 which
provides an overview of the agent’s decision-making flow. It
begins with route initialization using Dijkstra’s algorithm.
Once the simulation begins, the DQL agent continuously
monitors traffic conditions, computes Q-values using a neural
network and selects actions using balance exploration and
exploitation. After rerouting a vehicle, the agent receives a
reward and updates its policy through Q-Learning [25].

Fig. 3(a) illustrates the average speed comparison between
conventional routing and the DQL strategy. The DQL agent
achieves 6.3% improvement in average speed from
42.5km/h to 45.2 km/h. This improvement reflects the agent’s
ability to make real-time route adjustments in response to
dynamic traffic conditions. The increased speed is not a result
of aggressive driving but rather due to reduced congestion and
fewer stop-and-go patterns as learned through reinforcement
feedback on the systems. This performance boost
demonstrates the DQL algorithm’s capacity to optimize
vehicle trajectories for smoother flows and reduced delays. In
contrast, conventional methods that depend on static route
assumptions and lack flexibility to respond to evolving traffic
patterns, lead to reduced travel efficiency.

in 𝐶𝑂2 emission from 4273 g to 3811 g. These outcomes are
directly linked to improved driving stability as the DQL agent
learns to avoid high-traffic areas and maintains optimal
cruising speeds. Fig. 4(b) presents the economic implications
where the DQL approach reduces routing costs by 11% from
RM 45.20 to RM 28.50. These savings originate from lower
fuel usage, reduced idle time, and potentially less-wear-andtear on vehicle components. Table III consolidates these

The proposed DQL strategy consistently outperforms
conventional routing method across all key metrics as shown
in Table III. These improvements are the result of a robust
learning framework that adapts to traffic dynamics and
balances multiple routing objectives. The results validate the
use of DRL, especially in traffic route planning that
demonstrates its effectiveness in optimizing not just travel
efficiency, but also environmental and economic outcomes.
This positions DQL as a critical enabler in the development of
smart, adaptive, and sustainable urban transportation systems.

The consistent improvements across all measured
parameters align well with RL theory. The DQL agent
optimizes a reward function that encourages behaviors such as
maintaining efficient speeds, reducing fuel consumption, and
minimizing travel delays. This reward-driven lea rning
framework allows the agent to shift from initial exploration to
informed route exploitation as evident in the steady
improvements in fuel and emission metrics over time. The
proportional relationships between fuel consumption,
emissions, and cost also validate the agent’s ability to
generalize efficient routing behaviors across multiple
objectives as an essential trait in real-world of RL. The
simulation results demonstrate clear and actionable
implications for modern traffic route planning strategies. The
observed improvement in average speed under the DQL
framework directly contributes to reducing vehicle queue
times and overall travel delays which are the two critical Key
Performance Indicators (KPIs) in urban mobility systems.
These reductions translate into smoother traffic flow and
improved road network throughput which are essential for
enhancing commuter experience and public transport
efficiency. Beyond operational metrics, the significant
decreases in fuel consumption and 𝐶𝑂2 emissions align with
broader goals of sustainable urban development and climateconscious transportation planning.

V. CONCLUSION
The simulation results demonstrated that Deep QLearning (DQL) significantly outperformed conventional
fixed-path-based rerouting in reducing average travel time and
vehicle delay. The DQL model adapted effectively to dynamic
traffic conditions. These findings validate the effectiveness of
reinforcement learning in intelligent traffic management.
Future work is the integration of real-time traffic sensor data
and edge computing infrastructure is proposed to enable
deployment in live urban environments. This will bridge the
gap between simulation-based validation and real-world
implementation that will advance the applicability of adaptive
routing systems in smart city contexts.
