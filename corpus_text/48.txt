Abstract—In this paper, a multi-objective deep Q-learning
(MOM-DQL) method is developed for solving eco-routing
problem in a signalized traffic network. The problem is
formulated as dynamic multi-objective Markov decision
processes (MOMDPs). MOM-DQL can explore the optimal ecoroutes with respect to drivers’ different preferences on saving
travel time and fuel. The MOM-DQL agent is trained under a
series of learning environments that are based on historical
vehicle trajectories, fuel consumption, and traffic signal status
in the remote data center. The model that represents the action
value function of the historical dynamic driving conditions can
be downloaded to the vehicle requesting eco-routing service. The
model can quickly adapt to the most recent driving condition
through online one-shot learning and predict the optimal ecoroutes for the subsequent unseen driving condition of the
signalized traffic network. Simulation shows that the MOMDQL method can discover the optimal eco-routes, and saves
52% travel time and 33% fuel, compared to the shortest-path
strategy that is widely used in navigation systems.
Keywords—eco-routing, signalized traffic network, metalearning, reinforcement learning, deep Q-learning

I. INTRODUCTION
In the US, on-road vehicles consume about 24% of the
nation’s petroleum [1]. Research shows that route selection
can greatly affect vehicle fuel consumption and emissions [2,
3]. Finding the most fuel-efficient route can be formulated as
the eco-routing problem. The emergence of autonomous and
connected vehicles presents a timely opportunity to benefit
from eco-routing.
The previous eco-routing studies mostly rely on the
estimation of vehicle trajectories. Based on the estimated
trajectories, the costs in terms of fuel consumption/emissions
for all the links in the concerned traffic network are predicted
using microscopic fuel consumption models [4-7]. Then, the
shortest-path [4-6], or dynamic programming [7] methods are
used to find the eco-routes by minimizing the total cost or
expected cost respectively. However, these methods do not
consider the traffic signal information. The frequent stopand-go patterns caused by traffic signals essentially affect
vehicle trajectories and fuel consumption in a signalized
traffic network, especially in urban areas. Stochastic ecorouting [8] tackles the eco-routing problem by taking traffic
signals into consideration. The eco-routing is modeled as a
Markov decision process (MDP) with traffic signal status
included in the state definition. The estimated vehicle
trajectories are then used to predict the state transition
probabilities of the MDP.
There are two major concerns about these existing ecorouting studies, which may degrade their performances in
real-world scenarios. First, searching for eco-routes relies on
the estimated costs for all links of the entire traffic network.

Since the actual link based costs may vary over time, the
currently estimated link costs may not reflect the future
unseen dynamic driving conditions/environments. Second,
since a fuel consumption model is required to estimate the
cost, choosing an appropriate fuel consumption model that
can accurately estimate the fuel consumption cost is still an
open problem.
In this work we exploit advanced machine learning
techniques to effectively solve the eco-routing problem from
the following new perspectives. i) Rather than focusing on
accurately estimating vehicle trajectories, the historical realworld trajectories sampled from the dynamic traffic
environments are utilized to train a model that represents the
action value function through reinforcement learning (RL)
[9]. Therefore, the model has the capability of being
generalized during the training process and thus can
accurately predict eco-routes even in unseen states/traffic
conditions; ii) The estimated fuel consumption may not
always reflect the ground truth. We observe that the actual
measured instantaneous fuel consumption data of modern
vehicles can be easily accessed via on-board diagnostics
(OBD) ports and be collected through connected vehicles or
a mobile App; and iii) Rather than estimating the transition
probabilities of MDP with predicted vehicle trajectories, the
transition probabilities can be implicitly captured during the
training process with real-world vehicle trajectory data. This
will enable the model to better capture real-world traffic
conditions.
To develop such a RL method for eco-routing, there exists
two major technical challenges. First, in order to capture
dynamic traffic environments, training the RL agent with a
series of dynamic environments in sequential manner may
induce catastrophic forgetting on knowledge learned through
previous environments [10]. Second, to quickly adapt the
model to a new traffic condition/environment, the agent
needs to have fast online learning capability. However, for
the signalized traffic network that is very complex and has a
large state space, the RL usually demands long training time.
One promising realization of such a RL algorithm is
through meta-learning [11, 12]. Meta-learning is about
learning to learn. It can capture the distribution of a variety of
learning tasks. Thus it can solve a new learning task with only
a small number of training data, which is called few-shot
learning [11, 12]. Meta-learning can help the model adapt to
a new traffic environment quickly. In addition, since the
model has captured the entire distribution of training data
sampled from the dynamic environments, the catastrophic
forgetting can be addressed. On the other hand, it is
advantageous to formulate eco-routing as a dynamic multiobjective MDP (MOMDP) problem. Because different
drivers may have different budgets on travel time and fuel
consumption, they may have different preferences on the

II. RELATED WORK
To estimate the vehicle trajectories on each road link, the
eco-routing navigation system [5] incorporates multiple
sources of traffic information through a data fusion algorithm.
Based on that, the link-based energy/emission factors can be
computed as the navigation input [5]. In [6], the eco-routing is
considered as a constrained shortest-path problem. The
objective is to minimize the total travel time and fuel
consumption under the emission constraint. The emissions are
returned by their emission model that emphasizes vehicle
turning and idling impact, with estimated link travel velocities
as the input. With real-world vehicle trajectories, [7] develop
their fuel consumption model through Gaussian mixture
regression. The eco-routing is modeled as a travel time
constrained fuel consumption minimization problem. It also
concludes that the fastest-path, shortest-path and eco-route of
a given traffic network might be different. However, traffic
signal impact is not considered in those studies. The stochastic
eco-routing methods [8] estimates the vehicle trajectories with
link length and traffic signal status through a method
developed using traffic data. The estimation accuracy in
reality may be limited by the historical traffic data.
Recently, recurrent neural network (RNN) based deep
reinforcement learning has been applied to solve the vehicle
routing problem (VRP), which is the generalized travelling
salesman problem (TSP) [15]. The goal is to tackle the classic
NP-hard combinatorial math problem on a graph. It sheds light
on applying RL to routing problem. Imagination-augmented
agents (I2As) can learn to predict trajectories from a learned
environment model [16]. I2As are constructed with RNN and
convolutional encoder that processes trajectories. Therefore,
they are more applicable for video based applications.
Currently, there is no benchmark framework for multiobjective deep reinforcement learning . OLS [13] embeds the
DQL algorithm into an outer loop method. It addresses the
multi-policy task by finding the convex coverage set, which

contains complete optimal policies for the feasible weight
vectors. The decision values method [17] is proposed to
nonlinearly select actions. The priority parameters introduce
extra complexities to the algorithm.
From meta-learning aspect, the memory augmentation
method based on RNN is applied to learn the fast
reinforcement learning with recurrent policies [18, 19].
However, training RNN itself is usually time consuming.
Model-agnostic meta-learning is found to outperform the
recurrent approach regardless of the type of neural networks
[11], which is confirmed by a follow-up study [12] based on
[11]. Most of the existing meta-reinforcement learning
methods are based on policy gradient, which are more suitable
for continuous action space [11, 12, 18, 19].
III. MULTI-OBJECTIVE META-DEEP Q-LEARNING
To solve the eco-routing problem with reinforcement
learning, we propose to develop the MOM-DQL method. Data
communications are assumed available between vehicles and
a remote data center through connected vehicles or mobile
networks. The MOM-DQL is facilitated through the
interaction between the agent and a series of learning
environments that reflect the dynamics of the concerned
signalized traffic network. Each learning environment
consists of a graph with the link-based costs and traffic signal
data. Each link-based cost is obtained by averaging the costs
of all the vehicles that drive through the link over a short
period of time. The corresponding time-series traffic signal
status is logged as traffic signal data. 1) In the remote data
center, MOM-DQL trains the deep neural network model of
action value function offline through the agent interacting
with historical learning environments. 2) The vehicle that
requests eco-routing service downloads the model from the
remote data center. With the most updated learning
environment generated from the last short period of time, it
quickly adapts the model to the latest driving condition online,
which realizes the one-shot learning. 3) The resulting model
is applied to the concerned vehicle for its eco-routing for the
subsequent new real-world driving conditions.

dynamic traffic/driving conditions of the concerned

edges. The cost information for each graph contains the
average travel time and average fuel consumption for every
link. They are averaged over all the vehicles that have
traveled through the links in a short period of time (e.g., 5min). The link-based costs retain the cost variations over time
that exist in raw vehicle trajectories, which is more important
information than the actual costs to the agent. Also, using the
average link-based data instead of raw trajectories helps
model reduce overfitting during training. The traffic signal
status ‫ݎ‬௩ and intersection arrival time ‫ݐ‬௩ are directly logged in
time series for each graph.
The link based costs and corresponding traffic signal data
can be easily obtained through data collection enabled by

is eventually collected by the data center.

the link-based cost when the green light is on. The delay
segment mainly reflects the average delay cost at intersection

segment as the last 25 meters of a road link (ends at the center
of the intersection as shown in Fig. 1). The rest of the road

IV. EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS
To evaluate the performance of our MOM-DQL method,
simulation experiments are conducted. The algorithms for
MOM-DQL are implemented using Python and the deep
learning framework PyTorch. The environment module is
simulated with a widely-used traffic simulator called
Simulation of Urban Mobility (SUMO) [22].
The chosen signalized traffic network (Fig. 2) is from the
City of Pasadena, CA, USA, which is obtained from
OpenStreetMap [23]. It consists of 16 signalized
intersections, 18 two-way links, and 6 one-way links.
1,000 vehicles are simulated to travel on this network in 30
minutes. The trips of all simulated vehicles are randomly
generated with SUMO. For simulating the instantaneous fuel
consumption of each vehicle, the microscopic fuel
consumption model HBEFA [24] is used, which is also
available in SUMO. The costs of each link-based graph are
generated by moving average with a 5-minute window size
1-minute sliding step. In this paper, 5 contiguous link-based
graphs and their traffic signal data are used as learning
environments for offline MOM-DQL in the data center. The
number of sampled trajectories K = 5, minibatch size = 5,

V. CONCLUSION
In summary, we developed the MOM-DQL approach to
capture the dynamic traffic environments of a signalized
traffic network and explore the optimal eco-routes with
respect to different drivers’ preferences on travel time and
fuel consumption. With the downloaded model that
represents the action value function, the model can quickly
adapt to the latest traffic condition and perform online oneshot learning. The optimal eco-routes can be predicted under
future unseen driving conditions. Based on microscopic
simulation, our MOM-DQL can save 52% in travel time and
33% in fuel consumption compared to the Dijkstra method.
In the future, we will further test our method considering
additional realistic settings, e.g., complicated traffic
networks, diverse vehicle trip distributions, traffic jams, more
advanced fuel consumption models. We will also study if raw
vehicle trajectories can be directly used to fine-tune the
MOM-DQL model and further improve generalization. From
meta-learning aspect, it is expected to further generalize the
model on meta-level learner.
