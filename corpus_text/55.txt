Abstract— Urban traffic networks are efficient route design
problems due to the intricacy and dynamic character of
contemporary cities. Conventional methods hamper real time
adjustment to continually changing traffic conditions, thereby
providing suboptimal routes, a greater burden on the
roadways, and greater air pollution. This thesis presents a
novel deep reinforcement learning (DRL) method to optimize
the route planning for urban traffic networks. With this
rendering of real time traffic data, we use DRL to make
autonomous decisions to reduce travel time and congestion of
routes. One advantage of using sophisticated neural networks
for feature extraction and policy learning is that, since the
model is so adaptable to changing traffic patterns, the model
guarantees that. And, in comparison to common routing
algorithms, the results of simulations using real world traffic
data show that this method is able to greatly enhance route
efficiency and overall traffic flow management. The suggested
DRL based system is feasible as a smart city program based
on intelligent transportation system which can scale to grand
metropolitan regions. The research presents that DRL can
enhance the commuter run experience, reduce the
environmental effect, and alter urban transportation. Future
study will also investigate how this system can be better
optimized when integrated with traffic infrastructure enabled
by the Internet of Things.
Keywords: Deep Reinforcement Learning, Route Planning,
Urban Traffic, Traffic Optimization, Intelligent Transportation
Systems.

I.

INTRODUCTION

The growth of urbanization and increase of cars on the
road make cities' networks highly crowded especially the
road networks. Traffic congestion, as a major problem of
urban mobility, has negative impact on travel time, fuel
consumption, pollution and the quality of life [1]. Urban
planners and politicians increasingly worry about optimal
traffic flow, reduced congestion, and improved
sustainability of the transportation system. Intelligent
transportation systems (ITS) have gained promise as a
means of enhancing traffic management for cities using
cutting edge tech like AI and the Internet of Things (IoT)
and machine learning (ML) [2].

Route planning is the finding the best way that cars can
get from point a to point b while keeping their carbon
footprint down, their gas mileage up and trip time down —
it is what is one of the biggest problems in city
transportation. A limitation to traditional route planning
algorithms such as A* and Dijkstra's algorithm [3] is the
inability to adapt to changing traffic circumstances. In
heavily populated areas with unpredictable traffic
circumstances in real time, these techniques often lead to
less than ideal route selections [4].
Considering the recent progress in artificial intelligence
(AI) and an increasing number of researchers using the
reinforcement learning (RL) methods to improve urban
traffic systems route planning, this problem appears to be a
key research area. Reinforcement learning is well suited to
describe the dynamic and unpredictable urban traffic
network, letting agents learn optimum policies by
interacting with the environment [5]. However, traditional
RL algorithms are not capable of handling settings on a
large scale and high dimensional state spaces [6] and their
failure to achieve results in complex urban traffic situations.
Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) is a powerful Unsol
scheme that combines advantages of deep learning (DL)
with RL and evades such constraints. Deep reinforcement
learning (DRL) addresses high dimensional, large scales
settings by approximatively solving the value function or
policy through deep neural networks (DNNs) [7]. Hence,
DRL enjoys applications in areas with complex decision
making to be made in real time like autonomous driving,
robotics and gaming [8]. DRL for urban traffic management
presents the possibility for processing real time traffic data,
for adapting to changing traffic patterns and for providing
optimum route planning solutions with enhanced traffic
flow and reduced congestion. Based on DRL, this research
introduces a new method for improving the routing design
on an urban traffic network.
To reduce travel time, fuel consumption, as well as
congestion, the proposed solution aims to develop a real
time intelligent routing system that will change routes based
on traffic conditions.The system collects data using GPS
devices and (IoT) sensors and can provide users with

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
Efficiency of traffic flow in cities has been of great
interest for researchers in the area of transportation for quite
a long time. Traditional traffic management strategies, still
reliant on static models and data from the past, were
sometimes deficient at providing for the character of traffic
in metropolitan areas which changes so rapidly. Improving
efficiency in transportation networks and relieving
congestion in today’s traffic congested cities require more
importance in adaptive systems able to make choices online.
To respond to this need of mining ML and AI techniques
into traffic optimization and route planning [20].

A number of papers have looked into the idea of
optimizing route planning using RL. For example, Mousavi
et al. [11] have created an algorithm based on Q learning for
facilitating dynamic route planning within urban settings.
They showed that RL is indeed able to optimize traffic flows
by adapting to real time route optimization. It was only
applicable to smaller transportation networks though as
their method couldn't manage the complexity of larger
metropolitan systems. For instance, Chen et al. [12] used a
model-based RL algorithm to optimize traffic signal
management, but the proposed system was not meant for
real time route planning.

The first focus of traffic management efforts were static
routing techniques, with A* and Dijkstra's algorithm being
used as examples. For the good thing, these techniques are
all good in small, simple settings, but they can't make sense
of the complexity of real world urban traffic systems, where
roads are shut from time to time, accidents happen, and
demand itself is varying all the time. Static algorithms do
not take advantage of real time data (such as from GPS,
traffic sensors or Internet of Things devices) to make the
right running choices at the right time [21].

Recent developments of DRL have suggested
encouraging results to address these constraints. Li et al.
[13] explored a simulation of an urban setting which found
considerable reduction in traffic congestion through DRL
based real time traffic signal regulation framework. This did
not mean though, that DRL could not be used to make
decisions in real time, but their research only looked at
traffic signal management and not route planning. Another
piece of research used DRL for vehicle routing in a big city
traffic network, according to Liu et al. [14]. Their solution
beat previous algorithms for reducing travel time and
congestion, and shows that DRL is scalable to traffic
optimization in cities.

When AI and ML developed, scientists started thinking
about ways to optimize traffic flow with data methods.
Machine learning model's capability to learn in massive
datasets and produce forecast based on the present state of
traffic is enabling more adaptive traffic management
systems. Reinforcement learning (RL), which is a method
of machine learning in which an agent learns the best course
of action by seeing and responding to their environment, is
an important method. RL based approaches have able to
enhance traffic management by enabling systems to adjust
to the real time circumstances [22]. However, as is typical
of urban traffic systems with high dimensions and
continuous space, conventional RL methods fail when
applied [23].

Incorporating real-time traffic data and IoT enabled
infrastructure into the DRL reduces our likelihood of
obtaining a suboptimal solution by utilizing real-time
information. That’s where our method comes in. In contrast
to existing routing algorithms which rely on static or
historical traffic data [15], our system is more effective at
resulting in lower travel time and congestion in dynamic
urban areas, as it adjusts continuously to real time traffic
conditions [16]. Besides being practical, the proposed
approach addresses some of the social and ethical issues
associated with urban mobility.

To deal with these restrictions, researchers have taken
refuge in deep reinforcement learning (DRL). RL with deep
neural networks (DNNs) is known as DRL because DRL
integrates with RL to handle largescale environments and
approximate complicated functions [24]. In many
disciplines, such as autonomous driving, robotics, and
gaming, decision making is necessary in uncertain and
dynamic contexts, and DRL has been applied to
successfully solve such problems [25]. In applied to the
problem urban traffic management, we show that DRL can
significantly improve route planning by integrating real
time traffic data and progressively improving predictions.

Traffic congestion is negatively impacting pollutants in
the air, emissions of greenhouse gases and energy usage
[17]. We hope that our approach can contribute to the
greater sustainability of urban transportation systems and
better user experience by improving route planning and
reducing traffic congestion [18]. Furthermore, when using
AI &DRL to manage urban traffic, there are massive ethical
questions of privacy of data, justice and responsibility. The
legitimacy of AI driven transportation systems depends
upon transparency, equity, and alignment with society
values [19].

Several research has been conducted of the use of RL
and DRL for optimizing traffic. Mousavi et al. [26] applied
Q learning, a version of RL for urban dynamic route
planning. They found that traffic flow could be improved
when compared to more conventional methods. Their
method, though, can’t scale to large traffic networks
because the RL model is computationally expensive.
Moreover, Weietal. [27] also used a DRL model to optimize
traffic signal regulation in a simulated urban setting.

II.

METHODOLOGY

This research uses a Deep Reinforcement Learning
(DRL) approach to improve urban traffic networks’ route
planning. This traffic model has roadways as edges and
junctions as nodes. To go about it, the main goal is to find
an optimal route that requires the least amount of time and
side stepping the heavier traffic. A practical approach to
tackle this issue is using DRL. This technology enables
smart agents to learn the optimal paths by bringing real time
traffic information from several sources: the sensors in
internet of things (IoT), GPS devices, Traffic control system
etc. Data from traffic cameras, GPS devices and Internet of
Things (IoT) sensors are culled into preprocessed data.
Before this data can be fed into the model, it is cleaned,
removing noise and filling in any missing information, just
before this data is entered into the model. Because it is
updated with real time data regarding traffic conditions, the
DRL model is able to adapt routes for minimizing travel
time and congestion in real time.

In addition to the above mentioned, other recent research
investigated ways to integrate DRL for the Internet of
Things. To optimize routes in real time, Zhang et al [30]
proposed an (IoT) smart transportation system that uses
DRL.So that the DRL model could make educated
judgments about route planning, it had collected traffic data
(from IoT) sensors installed across the entire city. This
method did a great job in reducing travel time and
congestion, however this came at a cost of losing data
privacy and security, since those collecting and processing
huge amounts of data from the Internet of Things, generally
have their own reasons for collecting and wanting to process
said information, and there are rights not to be abused [31].
In recent years, transparency in justice concerns have
become paramount over AI driven traffic systems. For
example, Taddeo and Floridi [32]’s research acknowledges
some of the prejudice against which AI models could be
vulnerable and what this amounts to when used against
commuter categories. All of a sudden, the consideration
becomes all the more important when you’re trying to
optimize traffic flow: Judgments made by AI systems may
have outsized impact on specific parts of a city. Fairness is
needed to gain public confidence and give the promise of
fair results to all using the road, by means of AI driven
traffic optimization systems [33].

Dijkstra's algorithm. The DRL model also performs better
compared to these alternatives for reducing travel time,
managing congestion and scalability.

Here, we used Dijkstra and A* Algorithms.

the simulations when traffic circumstances changed
unexpectedly. As a result, these algorithms resulted in
greater congestion and longer time to destination for
vehicles utilizing these algorithms compared with vehicles
using the DRL based system.

IV.

CONCLUSION

[8]

In this research, we take a look at how we can use Deep
Reinforcement Learning to improve planning of urban
traffic routes overcoming some of the short comings of
other algorithms for example: A* or Dijkstra's. The DRL
model constantly adapts it work to current traffic conditions,
and minimizes the average travel time, fuel consumption
and congestion on large scale urban networks. By using real
time data from Internet of Things (IoT) sensors, GPS and
traffic cameras, the model learns from past traffic patterns,
and improves traffic flow management, more so during rush
hours. We compare the DRL system with existing
reinforcement learning approaches, standard technologies,
and other alternatives on efficiency, flexibility, and
scalability. It is helpful with reducing congestion and
making routes more efficient in making urban transportation
more sustainable. Thanks to the use of powerful neural
networks, the DRL model is able to manage complicated,
high dimensional traffic situations, and while the model
seems promising the obstacles to overcome remain.
Specifically, they include training computational needs and
requirements for high quality, real time data. To bring this
system to its next level, researchers may try to find ways
through the use of hybrid models, edge computing and
linked autonomous cars to enhance performance and
scalability further. The DRL method may be applied to
smart city transportation systems as a whole to contribute to
the paradigm shift towards greener, more efficient city
transportation..
