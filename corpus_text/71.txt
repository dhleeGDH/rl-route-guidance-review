Abstract—This paper presents the first attempt at an
emergency rescue path optimisation method using deep
reinforcement learning for urban industrial emergencies,
focusing on production safety accident-induced congestion. We
design a Prioritised Experience Replay Deep Q-Network
(PERDQN) with an attention mechanism and efficiently model
complex urban arterial road networks as a Markov Decision
Process. Our approach bases the traffic wave theory alongside
BPR functions to evaluate the impact of congestion at different
levels of control and assess the overall impact under various
traffic control mechanisms. Results of experiments on the Third
Ring Road of Beijing show that the proposed method reduces
emergency vehicle arrival time by 27.3% compared to
traditional algorithms, and 12.8% over standard DQN. The
congestion alleviation effectiveness of the method is claimed to
be superior in scenarios with more severe traffic, thereby
confirming that the method is optimally robust to changing
conditions of traffic.

This research develops an advanced path planning
technique employing the Markov decision process for
modelling urban roads while taking industrial accidentinduced congestion and vehicle queues into account [14]. In
our case, the PERDQN algorithm is used to optimise rescue
paths concerning various traffic management strategies [15].
The novelty of the work is examining models for urban
arterial roads, which are generally known for their
complicated structures, considering both network travel
efficiency and network effects by evaluating vehicle queuing
and arrival times using traffic wave theory and the BPR
function [16].
II. CONGESTED ROAD ENVIRONMENT MODELING
A. Urban Road Network Model
The complexity of urban road networks surrounding
industrial zones necessitates a systematic abstraction
approach to effectively model the intricate traffic dynamics
during production safety emergency situations. In this
research, we adopt a node-segment model to characterize the
urban arterial road network structure, which serves as the
foundation for subsequent reinforcement learning-based
route optimization. Urban arterial roads typically comprise
multiple components including main roads, side roads, and
various intersections that provide connectivity within the
network.

Keywords—Deep Reinforcement Learning, Emergency
Rescue, Path Optimization, Traffic Congestion, Attention
Mechanism, Prioritized Experience Replay

I. INTRODUCTION
Urban transportation networks are crucial in emergency
response; however, production safety accidents in industrial
areas cause severe congestion that can greatly delay
emergency vehicles, resulting in disastrous consequences
[1,2]. The intricate structure of the urban road network and
the constantly changing traffic conditions pose obstacles to
reaching the emergency vehicles within the required time [3].
Other types of path planning make use of the shortest path
algorithms, like Dijkstra or A*, which do not consider realtime traffic information or factors such as accidents and
traffic congestion modelling [4]. The use of distance metrics
devoid of the flow of vehicle traffic yield default routes that
are well below the target level [5]. These methods face
challenges in representing post-accident networks [6].

B. Introduction of Attention Mechanism
We refine the PERDQN framework by embedding an
attention mechanism to improve the perception capabilities
of important road nodes in emergency path planning. This
mechanism allocates importance weights concerning the
optimal path relevance on different road nodes. With the
implementation of a spatial attention layer that computes
attention coefficients αijα_{ij} αij for connected nodes, the
model can pay attention to important regions and disregard
less relevant information. The results of the experiments
show that this architecture performs 17.3% better in
accurately locating optimal rescue routes during industrial
accident-induced congestion, especially in complex urbanindustrial interfaces with numerous other viable paths. The
value of the attention mechanism is particularly striking when
algorithmic solutions are sought to be implemented in
congested urban environments with high traffic densities and
congestion in different parts of the region.

B. Comparative Analysis of Different Methods
Comparative analysis demonstrates that the PERDQN
algorithm significantly outperforms both traditional shortest
path algorithms and standard DQN in emergency rescue
scenarios. As shown in Figure 3, our method achieves a 27.3%
reduction in average arrival time compared to Dijkstra's
algorithm and a 12.8% improvement over standard DQN
across varying congestion levels. Most notably, the
performance advantage widens as congestion severity
increases, highlighting PERDQN's superior adaptability to
dynamic traffic conditions. The attention mechanism
integration proves particularly effective at complex
intersections, where the algorithm demonstrates a 34.2%
improvement in optimal decision-making compared to nonattention variants.

C. Decision Optimization under Multiple Control Schemes
Our research focuses on optimal decision making for
different control strategies applied during emergencies. The
four control strategies we examine include: Reverse Main
Road Control (RMC), Reverse Side Road Control (RSC),
Prograde Main Road Control (PMC), and Combined Control
Schemes. Through comprehensive simulations, it has been
determined that RSC is the most effective technique with a
performance increase of 23.4% in arrival times compared to
static routing given moderate levels of congestion. On the
other hand, RMC is the most advantageous when looking at
severe congestion scenarios since it offers routes around the
significant bottleneck areas. All vehicles are guaranteed the
best routing in real-time, particularly emergency vehicles,
through the traffic management control system, all thanks to
the PERDQN algorithm with attention which provides a
high-level context for selecting appropriate strategies based
on the control in use and traffic conditions.

C. Results Discussion
These findings affirm that the method we employed
optimally balances minimisation of arrival times with other
important traffic impact considerations, resulting in effective
yet sustainable paths for the broader road network. The
PERDQN with attention mechanism shows particular
strength in high congestion scenarios (0.75-0.80 ratio) where
traditional methods struggle, highlighting the algorithm’s
capability to circumvent severely congested areas with heavy
traffic. The statistical analysis performed validates the
significance of the aforementioned improvements in
performance (p < 0.01) for all levels of congestion analysed.
Although our method works remarkably well in highly
complex urban areas, we did notice slightly less pronounced
benefits in low congestion situations, which indicates that
some simpler approaches may suffice in free-flowing traffic
conditions.

V. CONCLUSION AND PROSPECTS
This research introduces a novel emergency rescue path
optimization method based on deep reinforcement learning
that significantly improves response times to production
safety accidents in congested urban-industrial environments.
The PERDQN algorithm with attention mechanism
demonstrates superior performance over traditional
approaches, achieving a 27.3% reduction in arrival time
while minimizing network-wide traffic impact. Despite these
advancements, limitations exist in computational efficiency
for real-time implementation and adaptation to extremely
large-scale road networks. Future research should focus on
extending this methodology to longer-distance urban
corridors spanning multiple districts, incorporating multiobjective optimization for scenarios with multiple
simultaneous rescue points, and integrating real-time traffic
prediction models to anticipate congestion development.
Additionally, exploring transfer learning techniques could
enable rapid adaptation to unfamiliar urban environments
without extensive retraining, potentially creating a
generalizable framework applicable across diverse urban
settings globally.
