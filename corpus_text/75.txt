Autonomous Vehicles in Smart Cities: a Deep
Reinforcement Learning Solution
Francesco Giannini Student Member, IEEE, Giuseppe Franzè Senior Member, IEEE,
Francesco Pupo Member, IEEE and Giancarlo Fortino Fellow, IEEE

Abstract—In order to alleviate the traffic congestion problem
in the context of Smart Cities, this paper proposes a new routing
strategy, based on Deep Reinforcement Learning. The idea is to
perform Routing actions in real-time while considering the actual
traffic situation on the overall road network.
To evaluate the performance of the proposed solution, a
co-design procedure, involving a microscopic traffic simulator
and MATLAB environment, is implemented allowing the use of
complex operating scenarios and the integration of information
coming from road maps and vehicle state trajectories.
Finally, some simulations performed considering a real city
district, show that the proposed algorithm can successfully realize
real-time routing decisions and improve accumulated waiting
time although the dynamic changes inside the road environment.

I. I NTRODUCTION
In the last decades traffic congestion has become a serious
issue and has led to an urgent need for innovative solutions
to deal with it. More specifically, Internet of Vehicles (IoV)
[1] [2] is spreading across all the high-tech roads and will
soon become the technological paradigm of assisted and
autonomous driving. In this context, Vehicle Routing Problem
(VRP) [3] is a well studied topic and several solutions have
been proposed: starting from classical techniques based on
optimization theory [4] [5] to the latest solutions based on
Game Theory [6] and Machine Learning [7] [8] [9].
In this paper, the application paradigm is sub-microscopictraffic-simulation [10]: each vehicle and its dynamics are
modeled individually. Therefore we deal with the problem
of routing and controlling the vehicles while maintaining a
platoon formation among them. Studies along these lines have
been presented in [11] and [12]. The context is a Urban Road
Network (URN), with its real traffic demand [13].
Our work uses Reinforcement Learning (RL) [14] to deal
with traffic congestion and to lead the autonomous vehicles
to their own destinations. RL learns from experience and
could reconsider its own believes about how to drive over the
URN, so it always tries to improve what it has learned. More

A. Proposed Solution: an overview
The problem has been addressed by means of a distributed
control scheme properly customized to exploit the capabilities
of the presented Deep Reinforcement Learning algorithm. In
particular, the idea we have developed consists in exploiting
routing decisions, provided by the DRL unit at each junction
occurrence, as set-point references for the underlying controllers, see figure 4.
Essentially, it consists of three units: a Path Planner
based on the DRL algorithm, a Reference Generator and
a Controller, each one tuned w.r.t. the involved vehicles
AV i , i = 1, . . . , L.
At each time instant t, the Path Planner receives the
measurements of the current position in terms of coordinates
and corresponding edge and the vehicles’ density on that edge,
from the generic vehicle AV i at t, and generates the action
ai (t) to be executed by the vehicle AV i at the next junction.
The latter translates into the set-point z i (t) for the underlying
control logic thanks to the Reference Generator unit. Hence,
the Controller computes an admissible command ui (t).

V. SUMO versus MATLAB CO - DESIGN
A co-design procedure is outlined in order to allow information sharing between SUMO and MATLAB. Essentially this
prescribes that, starting from the set of functions provided
by TraCI4Matlab® [17] that are customized to comply with
the DQL-Algorithm, the co-design in terms of software communication and data exchange is performed. More in detail,
the TraCI4Matlab toolbox allows the communication between
any MATLAB application and the SUMO traffic simulator.
TraCI application level protocol is based on the client-server
paradigm and it is built on top of the TCP/IP stack: the
application developed in MATLAB plays the role of a client
and it can access and modify the simulation environment
provided by SUMO that acts as a server. Hence, the proposed
co-design is in charge of automatically mapping all the URN
information into properly encoded MATLAB objects. Then, a
MATLAB graph is obtained by exploiting the SUMO xml files,

VII. C ONCLUSIONS
This work studies the Vehicle Routing Problem in the
context of Internet of Vehicles in order to decrease traffic
congestion, a issue recently intensified by the rapid growth
of vehicles number on the roads. In detail, we propose our
Deep Q Learning architecture with a micro-traffic simulations
approach.
The experimental results show that the proposal has good
performance in real scenario of traffic congestion and can
effectively reduce the accumulated waiting time. Future work
will consider how to accelerate distributed learning to get a
faster vehicle planning decision response and to deal more
specifically with the control problem.
