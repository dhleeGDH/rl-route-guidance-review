The routing decisions have gained an increasing relevance
because they allow to address as the way traffic is distributed
over a given network by significantly improving the traffic
efficiency. Such an issue has been attacked by resorting to
distributed machine learning schemes and combining historical and real-time traffic environment information to rationally
plan vehicle travel paths [12][13][14]. In this context, particular attention has been devoted to Reinforcement Learning
(RL) paradigm [15]. Accordingly some recent contributions
addressing the so-called Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP)
testifies the increasing interest acquired by this promising
research field, see [1][16][17].
In this paper the path planning problem for autonomous
multi-vehicle platoons, moving in URNs is faced by developing a DRHC architecture coupled with a Deep Q Learning
(DQL) scheme. Here, the idea consists in exploiting the
capability of a DQL approach to derive adequate routing
decisions in order to safely drive the platoon towards its target under the guarantee that collisions amongst the involved
vehicles never occur. Finally, simulations on a multi-agent
system of three point mobile agents are provided to show
the effectiveness of the proposed control architecture. Some
remarks conclude the paper.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section II
the path planning planning problem for urban road networks
is formulated. Section III describes the proposed control
architecture whose main feature is to combine into a unique
framework Deep Reinforcement Learning and distributed
RHC arguments.

Abstract— In this paper we deal with the control of platoon of
autonomous vehicles driving in Urban Road Networks (URNs).
We exploit the idea of using Deep Reinforcement Learning
(DRL) as the path planner of the proposed architecture. The
advantage of this solution is the capability to deal with the
actual traffic congestion while driving the autonomous vehicle
to its destination. In particular, the high-level routing decisions
are translated into manipulable set-points for Receding Horizon
controllers by making more computational affordable and
efficient the control action on the vehicle dynamics. Feasibility
and asymptotic closed-loop stability are formally proved. Some
simulations on a platoon, consisting of three agents described by
double-integrator models, are provided to show the effectiveness
of the overall architecture.

I. I NTRODUCTION
In recent years, regulating traffic flows with the aim to
alleviate congestion phenomena and keep road services at a
satisfactory level is an issue that has assumed an increasing
relevance. The most promising approaches for an intelligent
management of these aspects rely on the exploitation of
Internet of Vehicles (IoV) based paradigms, where each
vehicle is connected and, at least one roadside unit (RSU)
is deployed stationary on the road segment, see [1] and
references therein.
The coordination and control of multi-vehicle systems
have attracted the attention of several researchers in the last
decades as testified by relevant review contributions, see e.g.,
[2], [3]. More recently, the attention has been devoted to
analyze stabilization [4], [5] and path following [6] topics,
that in turn involve two relevant aspects: the characterization
of admissible state trajectories tubes for the current leader
and the design of ad-hoc control architecture to keep the
relative positions of the vehicles within safe and acceptable
limits.
Model Predictive Control (MPC) seems to be the most
adequate approach for dealing with this class of problems
in virtue of its intrinsic capabilty to explictly comply with
hard constraints.Hereafter, a brief review of some useful
contributions is given. In [7] a distributed receding horizon
control (DRHC) scheme for nonlinear platoons is developed.

V. C ONCLUSIONS
In this paper, a distributed control architecture, based on
the joint and cooperative use of DRL and MPC techniques,
has been developed to address the routing decision and
path planning problem for multi-vehicle platoons. As one
of its main merits, the resulting control scheme is capable to
identify admissible paths by avoiding undesired stop-and-go
behaviors of each involved vehicle. The simulation section is
instrumental to show the capability of the proposed approach
to properly take advantage of the routing decisions provided
by the DQL unit in order to better comply with the path
problem prescriptions.
