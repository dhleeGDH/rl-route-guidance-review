Path planning for vehicle platoons under routing decisions: a distributed
approach combining Deep Reinforcement Learning and Model Predictive
Control
Francesco Giannini, Giancarlo Fortino, Giuseppe Franzè and Francesco Pupo

Abstract— In this paper, the path planning problem under
routing decisions is considered for platoons of autonomous
vehicles moving in urban road networks. The key idea is
to exploit and adequately combine arguments coming from
two research fields: deep reinforcement learning and model
predictive control. Along these lines, a novel control architecture
is proposed and its feasibility formally proved. In particular,
the high-level routing decisions arising from the distributed
deep reinforcement learning operations are translated into
manipulable set-points for the underlying bank of receding
horizon controllers by making more computational affordable
and efficient the action on the vehicle dynamics. Finally, some
simulations on platoon, consisting of three agents described by
double-integrator models, are provided to show the effectiveness
of the overall architecture.

I. I NTRODUCTION
In the last decade, the increasing number of vehicles
has led to an urgent need for the innovative solutions to
deal with road traffic issues. More specifically, Internet of
Vehicles (IoV) is spreading across all the high-tech roads
and will soon become the technological paradigm of assisted
and autonomous driving, see the recent publication [1] and
references therein. In this context, routing problems are
among the most important and well-studied topics in network
control theory. The importance of traffic routing becomes
critical in several fields: power and information networks,
intelligent transportation systems and so on.
The coordination and control of multi-vehicle systems
have attracted the attention of several researchers in the last
decades a testified by relevant review contributions, see e.g.,
[2], [3]. More recently, the attention has been devoted to
analyze stabilization [4], [5] and path following, [6] topics,
that in turn involve two relevant aspects: the characterization
of admissible state trajectories tubes for the current leader
and the design of ad-hoc control architecture to keep the
relative positions of the vehicles within safe and acceptable
limits.
Along these requirements, the model predictive control
approach appears to be the more adequate to address this
class of problems that are by their nature subject to hard
constraints during the dynamical evolution. Hereafter, a

In recent years, the routing decisions issue in control
network theory has gained an increasing relevance because it
allows to address as the way traffic is distributed over a given
network by significantly improving the traffic efficiency. In
recent years, such an issue has been attacked by resorting
to distributed machine learning schemes and combining
historical and real-time traffic environment information to
rationally plan vehicle travel paths [12][13][14].
Starting from this analysis, in this paper the path planning problem for autonomous multi-vehicles, described by
double integrator dynamics and organized as a platoon,
moving in urban road networks is faced by developing a
distributed control architecture coupled with a distributed
deep reinforcement learning scheme. Here, the idea consists
in exploiting the capability of a deep reinforcement learning
approach to derive adequate routing decisions in order to
safely drive the platoon towards the pre-assigned target under
the guarantee that collisions amongts the involved vehicles
never occur. The latter can be done by resorting to predictive
ideas that, as well known, represent the better way to address
hard constraints during the system dynamical evolution. In
particular, a distributed receding horizon controller (RHC) is
designed whose one of the main feature is to take advantage
of the deep reinforcement learning outcome.
Finally, simulations on a multi-agent system of three point
mobile agents are provided to show the effectiveness of the
proposed control architecture.

III. D ISTRIBUTED DEEP REINFORCEMENT LEARNING
(DRL) SCHEME
The chosen routing decision approach is Q-learning. Qlearning is a method that avoids the need of a traffic evolution
model [15]. The Q-function Qi (s, a) is the expected total
discounted reward if the agent i takes the action a in the
state s and acts optimally thereafter. In each possible state,
if the training of the Q-function is correct, the best action
for the vehicle i is s.t. maximizes the Q-function. Deep
Reinforcement Learning is a reinforcement learning whose
Q-function is defined as a neural network (see [16]).
In a distributed framework, Deep Reinforcement Learning
is able to perform better decision than traditional routing
algorithms (see [1]). The algorithm works online and it can
deal with the actual traffic congestion condition. The aim
is to choose the proper action for each agent to alleviate
congestion on the entire URN.

Environment The environment in RL is everything that
is not the agents. The environment is the URN which is
traduced into a graph, see figure 3. The graph is translated
into a tree with the initial location as the root. The RL task
is the choise of the best action into the tree for each agent
in real-time.
