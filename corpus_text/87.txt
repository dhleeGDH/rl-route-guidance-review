Abstract—With the development of intelligent transportation
systems (ITS), providing efficient and robust path coordination
under complex disturbances has become an urgent challenge.
Traditional approaches often assume that broadcast instructions
can be fully received and strictly executed, overlooking the uncertainties introduced by broadcast reception failure and heterogeneous driver behaviors. For the first time, this paper incorporates
broadcast disturbances and behavioral response mechanisms as
external control signals into the path recommendation framework.
Probabilistic models are established to capture the stochasticity
in instruction reception and execution, while additional delay
estimation is integrated to construct an expected travel time
representation. On this basis, a Proximal Policy Optimization
(PPO)-based reinforcement learning framework is designed, where
multiple candidate paths together with the option of retaining the
current path are jointly considered in the action space, enabling
dynamic path recommendations that balance efficiency and risk.
Experiments based on real traffic datasets and road networks
verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, demonstrating
strong robustness under both peak and off-peak scenarios. The
results indicate that explicitly modeling broadcast and response
uncertainties, combined with reinforcement learning optimization,
provides a more practical and reliable solution for emergency
scheduling and efficient travel in ITS.
Index Terms—Path Recommendation, Intelligent transportation
systems, Reinforcement Learning, Broadcast Disturbance, Behavioral Response

I. I NTRODUCTION
The continuous growth in urban transportation demand has
rendered congestion and unexpected incidents critical bottlenecks limiting the operational efficiency of road networks.
Against this backdrop, coordinated path planning within Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) plays a pivotal role
in enhancing overall travel efficiency [1], [2]. Rather than
merely identifying the shortest path for individual vehicles, the
core objective lies in optimizing traffic resource allocation at
the system level, minimizing global delay, and improving the
robustness of the transportation network.
In typical traffic coordination practices, platforms often
employ broadcast-response mechanisms, such as information
dissemination and diversion guidance, to steer vehicles and enhance system wide efficiency. However, factors at the physical

communication layer, such as signal attenuation and interferenc,
may result in instruction delivery failures [3], [4]. Even when
instructions are successfully received, drivers may reject or delay execution due to distraction, individual risk preferences, or
other behavioral factors. These issues introduce a gap between
the theoretically planned model and actual traffic behavior,
ultimately undermining the effectiveness of the overall system.
These uncertainties become particularly pronounced in emergency scenarios. Take the example of guaranteeing the passage
of emergency vehicles such as ambulances or fire engines [5],
[6]. Even if the dispatch center is able to generate a priority
route and issue clearance instructions, vehicles ahead may still
fail to yield in time because of communication failures or
delayed driver responses. In sudden medical incidents or other
urgent situations, a common solution is to have traffic police
clear the way on site. Yet this method lacks a global view of
the route, works only by gradually dispersing traffic along local
segments, and depends on manual broadcasting and direction,
which inevitably slows the response. During peak hours or
within complex road networks, it often results in additional
delays. Such systemic breakdowns not only pose direct risks
to life and property but can also trigger cascading effects
that worsen local congestion. Therefore, a key challenge for
intelligent transportation research is how to explicitly model
the uncertainties arising from communication disruptions and
heterogeneous driver responses in coordinated path planning.
Existing research predominantly focuses on deterministic
path planning algorithms, such as shortest path [7] and dynamic traffic assignment [8], which perform well under ideal
conditions but struggle with random disturbances. Although
federated learning and reinforcement learning have recently
been applied to traffic signal control, path recommendation,
and flow allocation [9]–[11], most approaches still assume
full compliance with planned strategies, overlooking uncertainties from communication failures and driver behavior. In
emergency vehicle routing, prior studies mainly emphasize
priority scheduling and yielding mechanisms, often relying
on rule based heuristics or priority schemes, while neglecting
probabilistic modeling of broadcast disturbances and diverse

driver responses. Overall, the lack of systematic integration
of broadcast disturbances and behavioral response uncertainty
into coordinated path optimization frameworks significantly
limits both the robustness and the real-world applicability of

To address the above challenges, this paper models broadcast
disturbances and behavioral response uncertainty as an external
control signal, and explicitly incorporates their stochastic nature
through probabilistic modeling. Specifically, we not only construct reception probability and response probability models to
account for communication coverage insufficiency and driver
behavior differences, but also incorporate the estimation of
additional delay to build a probability weighted expected travel
time representation, which more accurately reflects path cost
under complex traffic conditions. Building on this foundation,
we further adopt a reinforcement learning approach based on
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Path switching and path
continuation are unified as decision options within a single
action space, enabling robust and risk aware coordination in
dynamic environments. The main contributions of this work
are as follows:
• We explicitly incorporate two critical factors, broadcast
disturbance and behavioral response uncertainty, into system level path coordination. By integrating traffic observation data with communication characteristics, we establish
a unified probabilistic model of reception and response
that captures the heterogeneity of command propagation
and execution. Unlike methods that rely on rigid prior
assumptions, our probabilistic modeling directly reflects
uncertainties in both communication and driver behavior,
making path coordination closer to real world traffic
conditions.
• To address additional delays and tail risks caused by
disturbances, we design a probabilistic delay estimation
mechanism. This approach maps uncertainties in message
reception and driver response into a distribution of additional delays and replaces the conventional mean based
travel time with a risk sensitive expectation. As a result,
path cost estimation becomes more realistic and more
sensitive to extreme outcomes.
• We propose a PPO based decision framework that integrates path switching and continuation within a single
action space. This design enables the policy to adaptively
balance efficiency and risk under dynamic traffic conditions, ensuring both global robustness and local stability.
The resulting path recommendation strategy provides a
practical and effective solution for coordinated optimization in intelligent transportation systems.
II. P ROPOSED M ETHOD
The proposed path recommendation method consists of three
core components: traffic feature modeling, disturbance and
travel time modeling, and risk sensitive policy optimization.
First, edge level traffic flow features are extracted from real

road networks and sensor data to construct a representation
of basic traffic conditions. At the disturbance modeling layer,
uncertainties arising from broadcast disturbances and behavioral responses are explicitly modeled and combined with additional travel time to construct a probability-weighted expected
travel time representation, thereby quantifying potential delays
caused by such disturbances. Finally, reinforcement learning
is employed for coordinated decision making, achieving a
balance between efficiency and risk in dynamic environments
and enhancing the robustness of path recommendation.

paper explicitly incorporates these two types of uncertainty into

C. Policy Optimization
In the path planning problem, the perception state of the
intelligent agent and the executable actions are the core of
policy optimization. This paper maps the road network features
at each time slot t into a state vector. The vector integrates
edge-level traffic flow, occupancy, speed, and the derived congestion coefficient, while explicitly introducing the broadcast
reception probability and the driver response probability. This
state representation can comprehensively reflect the road operation conditions and potential perturbation risks, providing
the intelligent agent with globally observable information for
decision-making.
At the decision-making level, the intelligent agent selects
one path to execute from K candidate paths from the origin to
the destination, and the action set is denoted as {1, 2, . . . , K}.
The action of keeping the current path is defined as the vehicle continuing on the predetermined route, whereas switching
represents the selection of a new candidate path by the intelligent agent. With the changes in traffic state and perturbation
feedback, the action set is dynamically adjusted.

III. E XPERIMENTS AND R ESULTS
This section outlines the experimental setup and results analysis, covering the dataset, parameters and evaluation metrics,
followed by a comparative performance analysis.
A. Experimental setup
1) Dataset and Road Network: The experiments are conducted using the publicly available California Performance
Measurement System (PeMS) dataset, which is widely adopted
in traffic prediction studies for its ability to capture dynamic
traffic behavior across various temporal scales. The dataset includes key indicators such as traffic flow, speed, and occupancy,
exhibiting strong spatiotemporal correlations and fluctuation
characteristics, making it suitable for congestion prediction and
path recommendation tasks. The road network is constructed
based on OpenStreetMap (OSM), and sensor monitoring data
are spatially matched and mapped to corresponding road network edges.
2) OD Pair Construction: Based on the constructed road
network, the experiment first generates origin-destination (OD)
pairs covering different regions and travel distances. All OD
pairs are then split into training, validation, and test sets with
a ratio of 70% : 15% : 15%.
3) Parameter Settings: For each OD pair, K = 15 candidate
paths are generated. The main hyperparameters of the PPO
algorithm are set as follows: learning rate lr = 0.0003,
γ = 0.99, λ = 0.95, ε = 0.2, and epoch = 200. The reward
function considers both traffic efficiency and communication
disturbance. The travel time cost is calculated based on the

expected travel time, while the disturbance term is incorporated by modeling the broadcast reception probability and
the non-response probability, introducing weighted penalties.
This ensures the reward signal reflects both efficiency and
communication stability.
4) Evaluation Metrics: To comprehensively evaluate the
performance of the proposed path recommendation method, the
following metrics are employed.
• Average Travel Time (ATT, s): Measures the average
time required for vehicles to complete the task in a given
scenario, reflecting the overall traffic efficiency.
2
• Variance of Travel Time (s ): Represents the dispersion
of travel times, where a smaller variance indicates more
stable travel outcomes.
• Maximum Delay (s): Maximum additional delay relative
to expected time, indicating worst-case or extreme congestion risk.
• Switching Frequency: Average number of path changes
per decision step, used to assess policy stability and
switching cost.
• Task Completion Rate: Proportion of trips that reach
the destination within the allowed time window, indicating
reachability and reliability.
• Quantile Travel Time (P50 / P90 / P95 / P99): Corresponds to the travel times at different quantiles. P50
denotes the median, reflecting typical performance under
normal conditions, while P90, P95 and P99 are employed
to evaluate tail performance, capturing travel delays under
highly congested or abnormal conditions.
By jointly analyzing the above metrics, this study evaluates
the proposed method from the perspectives of efficiency, stability, robustness, safety, and reliability, thereby providing a
comprehensive assessment of its effectiveness under different
parameter settings.
B. Experimental results analysis
1) Peak vs Off-Peaks: Figure 1 presents the performance
comparison under the Off-peak and Peak traffic scenarios.
The algorithm is evaluated across five dimensions: ATT, Var,
Maximum Delay, Switching Frequency, and Task Completion
Rate.
First, in terms of traffic efficiency, the average travel time in
the Peak scenario is significantly higher than that in the Offpeak scenario, indicating that congestion leads to an overall
decline in efficiency. At the same time, the variance of travel
time nearly doubles under the Peak condition, suggesting
greater fluctuations and enhanced uncertainty in travel outcomes. Second, regarding robustness, the maximum delay in
the Peak scenario reaches 360.65s, approximately three times
that of the Off-peak scenario, which demonstrates that agents
may encounter more severe delays under extreme congestion.
This result reflects the cumulative effect of traffic disturbances
in peak conditions. In terms of behavioral characteristics, the
switching frequency during the Peak scenario is about twice
that in Off-peak, implying that agents adopt more aggressive

Figure 2 illustrates the comparison of travel times at different
quantiles (P50, P90, P95, P99). It can be observed that, overall,

extreme peak conditions. In addition, the Off-peak curve is
relatively smooth, suggesting a more concentrated distribution
of travel times and better system stability, while the Peak curve
is steeper, implying that vehicles in congested environments
are more susceptible to random disturbances and thus exhibit
greater uncertainty.
In summary, the experimental results demonstrate that the
proposed system is able to maintain satisfactory travel efficiency and stability under varying traffic conditions. In typical
scenarios, it effectively reduces travel time, reflecting high
operational efficiency. Under congested and extreme conditions,
although efficiency and robustness decline to some extent and
agents incur higher lane-changing costs to sustain performance,
the system successfully suppresses excessive tail delays. Moreover, the overall task completion rate remains close to 100%,
further confirming the adaptability and robustness of the method
in complex traffic environments.

C. Summary
This chapter validates the effectiveness and robustness of
the proposed strategy under varying traffic conditions through
comprehensive experiments. By explicitly modeling broadcast
disturbances and behavioral responses and integrating PPO for
online decision-making, the system achieves high task completion rates in both off-peak and peak scenarios. At the same time,
it reduces average travel time and controls tail risks, showing
strong adaptability to dynamic traffic environments. Although
peak-hour conditions reduce efficiency and increase delay fluctuations, the overall performance remains stable, indicating
robust behavior. Sensitivity analysis further reveals that policy
performance depends on the interaction of speed, horizon, and
memory, and that appropriate parameter choices can balance
efficiency and stability. Moderate updates, greater emphasis
on long-term returns, and intermediate GAE settings yield the
most consistent results. Overall, the method shifts from mean
optimality to risk-aware optimization, achieving a good balance
between efficiency and robustness while supporting deployment
in complex traffic environments.
IV. C ONCLUSION
This paper proposes a reinforcement learning based path
recommendation strategy that incorporates broadcast disturbances and behavioral responses, addressing the challenges
of reception uncertainty and driver variability in intelligent
transportation systems. Communication coverage insufficiency
and driver compliance are modeled as independent probabilistic
events, and a risk aware expected travel time is constructed
to provide a more realistic and robust cost representation
for path planning. The candidate path set is optimized with
the PPO algorithm through online learning, enabling efficient
and resilient decision making under complex disturbances.
