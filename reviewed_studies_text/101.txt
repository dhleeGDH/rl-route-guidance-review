Abstract—The rapid proliferation of electric vehicles (EVs)
has necessitated intelligent route planning systems that
prioritize energy efficiency over mere distance or travel time.
This study proposes a novel Energy Optimal Route Planning
Framework that integrates multi-source spatio-temporal data
with hybrid artificial intelligence (AI) models to enhance
navigation decisions for EVs. The artificial intelligence
approach includes the graph-based representation of roads,
physics-directed neural differentiable representations of realtime energy prediction, and quantum-inspired optimization of
multi-objective cost optimization. An extraordinary hybrid
learning system is introduced that integrates Deep
Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and Swarm-Based Global
Search to enable the learning to be dynamic in response to the
traffic, elevation, and charging constraints. The system also
involves Gated Graph-Temporal Convolutional Networks (GGTCNs) to predict traffic trends in order to proactively route
traffic. A city-wide experimental validation shows that 26.3%
energy efficiency is improved, 14.8% route detour time
reduction, and 91.72 route selection accuracy improve
compared to the traditional shortest-path and time-based
navigation models. The future of smarter EV routing systems is
that the framework has real-time flexibility, predictive
intelligence, and multi-objective optimization. This study
provides a foundation of intelligent transportation systems that
are energy-sensitive, infrastructure-sensitive and appropriate in
the real-world application in the highly populated or
infrastructure-restrained.

I. INTRODUCTION
The transition toward sustainable transportation is
reshaping the automotive industry, with electric vehicles

(EVs) emerging as a key component in reducing carbon
emissions and promoting energy efficiency [1]. With the
increasing spread of EVs in the world, supported by the
development of the battery technologies and the incentives
offered by the government, there has been a growing
necessity of creating intelligent navigation systems that
would suit the needs of EVs [2]. Compared to traditional
internal combustion engine automobiles, EVs are limited in
range of battery, have extended charging times, and have a
low density of charged station networks. EVs therefore must
rely on route planning that extends beyond paradigms of
shortest-distance and fastest-time optimization to enable
energy optimization, real-time traffic prediction and
awareness of charging infrastructure [3].
EVs routing is a multi-objective problem that is energyefficient in nature, entailing minimizing the battery used,
travel and charging time, and battery health [4]. The fact that
the issue is exacerbated by external factors like dynamic
traffic jam, road elevation, weather changes, and charging
stations also complicate a solution [5]. These variables
present a demand of real-time decision-making structures that
are adaptive and able to process non-homogeneous data and
learn the best routing policies through constant interaction
with the environment [6]. Moreover, predictive modeling can
be essential to prevent congestion proactively and be able to
plan possible routes within battery limits, particularly in big
cities with unstable traffic patterns [7].
The majority of the available EV routing models are
based on classical algorithms such as Dijkstra- or Astarbased, and are not flexible to changes in traffic, elevation, and
battery state [8]. Although machine learning enhances energy
and traffic forecasting, it tends to run alone resulting in partial

II. RELATED WORKS
To overcome constraints of distance-based or time-based
routing, an energy-optimal routing system of electric vehicles
was provided. A neural network based on LSTM estimated the
state-of-charge consumption by using real-world driving data
and using the spatiotemporal characteristics of speed,
temperature, road gradient, and distance [11]. The model also
modeled nonlinear and time-dependent energy patterns
without the use of complicated physical parameters. Systems
routing map, traffic, weather, and charging data were
incorporated into a weighted graph and an improved version
of A+ was used. Experiment findings revealed much better
accuracy compared to traditional and commercial solutions.
Due to the growing requirement of decarbonization and
smart transportation, the technologies of eco-driving of
electric vehicles were developed. An integrated approach of
macro traffic flow, detailed vehicle energy models and road
environmental factors was implemented to come up with a
hierarchical eco-driving framework [12]. Real road topology
traffic network was built and energy-efficient routes were
determined with the help of multi-heuristic A 3 algorithm.
Convex optimization allowed velocity planning to be used
accurately along chosen routes. It was experimentally
demonstrated to incur computation times of less than 2
seconds and energy and time savings of over 10 %.
Increasing environmental consciousness and the issue of
climate change had risen the use of electric vehicles, and the
governmental incentives were a part of this rise. Nevertheless,
range anxiety was still a significant impediment because
driving range was short, charge times were lengthy and
charging stations were widespread [13]. A progressive route
planning strategy was introduced with battery state of health,
external temperature, and driving style being considered in
routing. Actually, available energy was used to select
reachable charging stations. The effect of the strategy
proposed on the results of simulation indicated high risk of

battery depletion in situations where routes were based on
proclaimed range alone which became effective.
Electric vehicles powered by fuel cells needed to have
proper energy management policies to achieve the sustainable
transportation gains. The framework of co-optimization was
advanced to plan the speed and energy consumption of
intelligent fuel cell electric vehicles [14]. A Toyota Mirai
energy model has been designed and tested on the basis of
real-life test data. The optimal control problem was to
minimize hydrogen use, prevent peaks of power and
maximize regenerative braking subject to battery and motor
limits. Test results demonstrated that a 25 % fuel saving was
achieved on flat roads and a 36 % increase in results when
driving under realistic scenarios.
The movement of electricity cars and their charging
patterns had an impact on the functioning of power grid and
road traffic. The graph theory, time-varying traffic
information and a road impedance formulation were applied
to create a dynamic road network model [15]. The electric
vehicle spatio-temporal travel properties were modelled and a
modified Dijkstra algorithm with real-time impedance used to
represent the energy-consumption as accurate. Real scenario
simulation outcomes indicated that it reduced travel time by
18 %, load balance in each charging station, stabilization of
voltages within 7 % and congestion at each station.
III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
3.1. Spatio-Temporal Traffic Data Acquisition and MultiSource Integration
The methodology starts with the total acquisition and
synthesis of spatio-temporal data which plays a critical role
in proper and contextual route planning of the electric
vehicles (EVs). These data are in many non-homogenous
sources like vehicle GPS tracking, Internet of Vehicles (IoV),
stationary sensors and live traffic applications (Google Maps,
HERE Maps) and open geospatial databases like
OpenStreetMap and Waze. Besides moving traffic, data on
the availability of charging stations, altitude of the ground,
and twistiness of the roads, as well as the speed limit, is also
absorbed. The system uses real time data streaming using the
MQTT protocols and edge based preprocessing modules to
normalize formats and timestamps. Spatial harmonization
process is done with the Delaunay triangulation in order to
maintain a uniform mapping of the sparse or unbalanced data.
The temporal aggregation is then accomplished through the
sliding window model that realizes that the updates at high
frequencies are synchronized without overloading the
system. The space is partitioned into uniform cells with
hexagonal hierarchical indexing (via Uber H3) to provide
efficient indexing, caching and look-up of the dynamic
routing engine. The combined data constitutes the input of
energy-sensitive route optimization.

3.4. Multi-Criteria Cost Function Formulation with
Quantum-Inspired Weight Optimization
In order to be able to incorporate the various goals in EV
routing, such as minimal energy consumption, minimal time,
minimal battery degradation, and minimal charging detours,
a composite cost functional is formulated based on the
weighted-sum of normalized parameters. The parameters
involve the amount of energy consumed by each segment, the
approximate time taken, the number and length of charging
stations, and closeness to the degradation limits. Rather than
manually giving a weight to these criteria, a QuantumInspired Evolutionary Algorithm (QIEA) is used to maximize
them. This metaheuristic algorithm uses the simulated
quantum parallelism with the use of the probability amplitude
representations in which a high and complex solution space
is explored using fewer generations. Interestingly, the QIEA
dynamically adjusts weights to various route profiles and
preferences of users, like urgency or battery health priority. It
means that the cost function can be used to conform to
different use-cases, and then have a personalized
optimization instead of a one-size-fits-all strategy.

3.6. Multi-Agent Swarm-Based Search for Global
Optimal Routes
The framework is used to investigate the large and
evolving routing space subject to energy and time constraints
through the deployment of the hybrid optimization model,
Particle Swarm Optimization with Dynamic Neighborhoods
(HPSO-DN). Every swarm agent is the candidate route path
in the form of a sequence of nodes and transitions. The swarm
is optimized through successive evolution and local
exploitation (through dynamic neighborhood best) and global
exploration (through velocity updates). An example of a
fuzzy heuristic element is introduced to optimize the use of
the Ant Colony Optimization (ACO). In this case, the
transition probabilities are adjusted depending on the energy
penalties imposed by the terrain and coverage of the charging
stations. The two-step process enables the swarm to get out
of local optima and find globally optimal paths that not only
reduce cost, but also offer plausible backup paths in the event
of unexpected congestion or charging unavailability. Swarmbased system is also parallelizable in nature and can run
efficiently on a multi-core system or cloud-based system.
3.7. Proposed Model: Hybrid AI Framework Combining
Deep Reinforcement Learning with Graph Optimization
The climax of the methodology will be the fusion of a
hybrid artificial intelligence system that integrates Deep
Reinforcement Learning (DRL) and real-time graph
optimization heuristic. According to DRL component, the
component is trained using the Deep Q-Network (DQN) to be
able to learn the best navigation policies by simulating
interaction with the environment, where reward function
serves as an expression of energy efficiency, time
minimization, and battery health preservation. The agent
monitors the existing road condition, battery and traffic
prediction in order to make the decision to follow, i.e., the
change of route. The system uses an experience replay
mechanism in which the events that occur infrequently and
dissipate energy are sampled more to hasten learning in

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The main working concept of the suggested energy
optimal route planning tool is the possibility to calculate the
most efficient energy-saving and time-saving path of electric
vehicles dynamically, combining traffic, real-time
information regarding the consumption of energy, and
charging infrastructure. The system builds a spatio-temporal
road graph and forecasts traffic congestion based on graph
temporal models, and predicts battery drain in segments
based on physics-informed neural energy model. These
forecasts are provided to a multi-objective optimization
engine which combines reinforcement learning and swarmbased search to find global optimum paths. The last chosen
path changes constantly based on the environment and
transportation, which provides viable practical and
trustworthy energy-conscious navigation.

V. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE
In this paper, the authors introduced a hybrid artificial
intelligence approach to energy-optimal route planning on
electric vehicles, combining graphics with graph-based
optimization methods with deep reinforcement learning. The
proposed system can successfully operate in real-time to
model traffic conditions and energy use along with charging
constraints by utilizing multi-source spatial and temporal
data. This method is characterized by the use of neural
differential equation to estimate the energy and to optimize
the multi-objective cost function inspired by quantum, which
is why it is not similar to the current solution of routing with
rules or distance as a priority. The combined framework
attains a prediction performance average of 91.72% of
accurate prediction of routes, and provides better results in
energy consumption, detour reduction, and congestion
reduction in a variety of traffic conditions. The framework
proposed has very high potentials to be implemented within
the real-world intelligent transport systems especially in

urban areas where the availability of charging and traffic
congestion is a major limitation. The next steps of this work
will involve vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) communication
to support decentralized planning, federated learning to
maintain the privacy of users among a group of EV agents,
and introduction of weather-aware dynamics into energy
models. In addition, smart grid infrastructure can be
integrated with adaptive pricing that can further optimize the
charging decisions. In the end, the framework provides a
solution of next-generation electric mobility, which is
scalable, accurate, and adaptive to provide its contribution to
sustainability and smart planning of infrastructure in cities.
