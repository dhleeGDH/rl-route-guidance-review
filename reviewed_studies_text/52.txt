ABSTRACT
Real-time route planning is a challenging work for the Internet of Vehicles (IoV) systems. Some people
have used swarm intelligence algorithms to schedule routes for connected vehicles. These algorithms are
always sensitive to the setting of initial parameters. These days, reinforcement learning algorithms to
some extent overcome this drawback. However, previous research based on reinforcement learning has not
taken into account the real-time variation in the number of vehicles on each lane. In this work, a new
reinforcement learning algorithm, named dynamical-weighted Q-learning (DWQL), is proposed. This approach
plans the routes for connected vehicles by introducing dynamic weighting factors into Q-learning algorithm.
By employing dynamic weighting factors, the algorithm selects the optimal route for connected vehicles based
on their current positions as well as congestion status of the accessible roads. The experimental results show
that compared to existing algorithms, the DWQL algorithm proposed in this paper can effectively reduce the
duration, time loss, waiting time, economic cost, and improve the traffic efficiency of the IoV system.

1. Introduction
The Internet of Vehicles (IoV) has drawn more and more public
attentions these days [1–3]. The idea of IoV originates from the Internet of Things (IoT), and it is considered one of the most promising
technologies in IoT technology [4]. The IoV system collects data by
using a sensor network, establishes communication between connected
vehicles through wireless communication technology [5,6], and processes the needs of drivers through advanced technologies such as
fog computing, big data processing and reinforcement learning [7].
The IoV technology is considered the cornerstone of future urban
transportation [8,9].
Dynamic route planning is a crucial research area in the IoV domain,
and several noteworthy studies in this field deserve attention. For
instance, Shao et al. used the multi-objective evolutionary algorithm
to solve the multi-objective vehicle routing problem with random
demand. In the process of evolution, the radial basis function (RBF)
network was introduced to identify potential knowledge of individuals.
With the invert generation distance (IGD), the number of vehicles,
and the driver remuneration acted as the evaluation indexes, after
weighting the summation of all the metrics, the results improved by
11.33% compared to other algorithms [10]. Kanoh applied a genetic
algorithm of virus infection to navigate vehicles. When traffic jams
occur during vehicle driving, virus genetic algorithm was deployed to
choose alternative routes [11]. Lai et al. used the hybrid evolutionary
algorithm to solve the dynamic route planning problem and tested it

on a multi-intersection map network in Singapore. The results proved
that the hybrid evolutionary algorithm for solving the dynamic route
planning problem (HEADRPP) had good robustness regarding the dynamic route planning problem [12]. Jung et al. used the ant colony
optimization (ACO) algorithm to solve the dynamic route problem in
the IoV environment [13], the algorithm have been proven to have the
ability to prevent a great number of vehicles from gathering on one
road and causing congestion. Lin et al. used multi-objective particle
swarm optimization (MOPSO) algorithm to the optimal route planning
of underwater robots. The results demonstrate that dynamic weighting
yields higher efficiency and better stability compared to fixed weighting [14]. However, these swarm intelligence algorithms have numerous
initial parameters, requiring significant manual adjustment effort, and
are prone to achieving local optimal solutions [15,16]. In comparison
to swarm intelligence algorithms, reinforcement learning algorithms,
such as deep Q-network (DQN) and Q-learning, have relatively fewer
parameters, which allows for convenient parameter adjustment, and
yielding solutions of relatively higher quality [17–20].
Benefiting from the increased computational power of computers,
reinforcement learning is used to solve problems in many fields [21,
22]. Zhou et al. developed a new algorithm based on the multi-agent
advantage actor critic (MA2C) framework. The results show that this
algorithm outperforms other existing algorithms in terms of efficiency,
safety, and driver comfort indexes [23]. Yang et al. proposed a new
reinforcement learning algorithm efficient transfer actor–critic (ETAC)

(1) When a vehicle passes the intersection, the broadcast mode is
used to initialize the request. First, to determine the road 𝑅𝑛 corresponding to the vehicle’s location, the communication mode, which is
fixed as V2I, is applied to communicate with the infrastructure. In this
step, other vehicles or agents in the network will not respond after
receiving the message. Then V2 A is used to interact with the agent
to determine the next action 𝑎𝑡 of the vehicle, and it will execute this
action as soon as possible;
(2) The infrastructure is in the center of the intersection. When the
infrastructure receives the request, the operation is determined by the
parameter 𝑚. If it is a V2I interactive mode, the infrastructure directly
sends back the road 𝑅𝑛 in that direction. In case of A2I interaction
mode, the infrastructure counts the number of vehicles in all directions
separately 𝑁𝑐 and sends the different values back to the agent;
(3) The agent is responsible for planning the route in a specific
area. Upon receiving V2 A information, the agent uses the road name
𝑅𝑛 to determine whether it is within the managed area under its
governance. If not, it will not respond. However, if it is within the area,
the agent performs the route planning for the connected vehicle using
the algorithm proposed in this work.

the action space is discrete and limited. Second, if the area controlled
by the agent is too large, the amount of computation required will
increase exponentially, which in turn will affect the algorithm efficiency. Therefore, the table-record method is more suitable for this

66.3% (Dueling DQN), and 25.4% (DDQN) per vehicle, respectively.
Under the large-scale road network, TS and DDQN were removed
due to their poor performance. Moreover, the time loss index of the
DWQL algorithm is reduced by 79.31% (ACO), 76.84% (DQN), and
86.43% (Dueling DQN) per vehicle, respectively, whereas the duration
is reduced by 74.93% (ACO), 76.13% (DQN), and 73.06% (Dueling
DQN) per vehicle, respectively. It can also reduces the waiting time
index by 58.7%, 44.8% and 69.3%, respectively, and the economic cost
decreased by 53.5% (ACO), 60.6% (DQN), and 53.2% (Dueling DQN)
per vehicle, respectively.

scenarios, it has the potential to significantly reduce the likelihood
of traffic congestion. In situations where congestion occurs due to
high traffic flow, the algorithm can assist connected vehicles in swiftly
leaving congested road networks and reaching their destinations. This
has the potential to greatly improve traffic efficiency in the era of IoV.
