A Sustainable Multi-Agent Routing Algorithm for
Vehicle Platoons in Urban Networks
Francesco Giannini , Student Member, IEEE, Giuseppe Franzè , Senior Member, IEEE,
Francesco Pupo , Member, IEEE, and Giancarlo Fortino , Fellow, IEEE

Abstract— In this paper, a sustainable routing algorithm for
vehicle platoons operating in smart urban networks is presented.
The proposed approach makes use of deep reinforcement
learning (DRL) and set-theoretic model predictive control (MPC).
In particular, the learning process aims at reducing traffic
congestion and C O2 emissions, whereas the MPC unit allows
to adequately track the assigned path by using real-time traffic
data. To adequately analyze the performance of the resulting
control architecture, the SUMO and MATLAB environments are
used to implement complex operating scenarios where road maps
data and vehicle state trajectories can be shared and exchanged.
Finally, numerical studies are provided by resorting to the SUMO
environment and considering a platoon of five vehicles. The
resulting simulation campaign puts in light the capability of the
training process to significantly mitigate the C O2 emissions of the
whole platoon: from a minimum of 3.7% to a maximum of 13%
with respect to the use of the well-known Dijkstra algorithm.

I. I NTRODUCTION
N RECENT years, sustainable transportation issues have
gained an increasing interest mainly for their capability
to have deep socio-economic and environmental impacts in
the human life across the world, see e.g., [1], [2], and [3].
Accordingly, novel intelligent vehicle transportation systems
play an important role: from one hand, they are of help in
reducing traffic undesired effects on the environment care and
human being health, while from the other hand, they are in
charge to improve the city long-term sustainability [4].
In this context, a formal and well-established way to evaluate the city sustainability level relies on the characterization
of the so-called triple bottom line (TBL) index. As pointed
out in [5], such a criterion concerns with environmental,
economic and social aspects. In particular, socio-economic
indicators can be defined from different point of views,
to cite a few see [6]: the “business profit”, the purchase of
goods and services, the imposed taxes and so on. Whereas,

A. Literature Review
In the last decade, several innovative solutions have
been developed with the aim of addressing environmental
sustainability and traffic congestion specifications [11].
As a matter of fact, today’s vehicles are also connected
devices [12], [13] and Internet of Vehicles (IoV) paradigm
has been successfully applied in high-tech roads scenarios
as testified by [14] and [15] and references therein. It is
worth underlining that the IoV technology can provide vehicle
services oriented to sustainability goals, such as information
about traffic congestion and C O2 emission levels.
By referring to the field of autonomous vehicle systems,
the Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) [16] is by now relevant
as testified by the significant number of strategies conceived
for its solution: ranging from optimization-based techniques [17], [18] to Machine Learning [19], [20], [21], [22].
By considering the more recent advancements, an interesting
contribution on the adopted practices for dealing with the
sustainable urban vehicle routing problem is presented in [23].
There, a carefully analysis of different routing problem
customizations, complying with mathematical models, sustainability levels and proposed solutions, has been provided.
Moreover, the contribution [24] focuses on the so-called
vehicle routing problem with backhauls (VRPB): a variant of
the VRP that analyzes the vehicle efficiency versus the time
interval of use. Conversely, the traffic-induced air pollution is
addressed in [25], where an innovative scheme for detecting

B. Main Contribution
In this paper the main aim is to propose new insights
in the field of path planning for constrained autonomous
vehicles moving in the cluttered environments of urban road
networks. Preliminary studies along these lines have been
presented in [37], [38], and [39]. In particular, the attention
will be devoted to address the Sustainable Urban Vehicle
Routing (SUVR) problem. To this end, the guideline is to
make the resulting control architecture scalable, flexible and
computational low demanding in order to be capable of
addressing time-varying operating scenarios along the path.
In particular, the vehicles along the path are organized as
platoons in order to improve as much as possible sustainability
features of the overall architecture. From a methodological
perspective two aspects deserve particular attention:
1) the computation of routing decisions for mitigating traffic congestion phenomena and reducing C O2 emissions;
2) a constrained control strategy in charge of adequately
exploiting the routing decisions for sustainability
purposes.
These considerations naturally lead to consider a distributed
framework, necessary to fulfill scalability and flexibility
specifications, where the first issue is addressed by resorting
to an innovative DRL scheme, while the point 2) is solved
by means of a MPC approach adequately designed to reduce
as much as possible computational loads pertaining to the
underlying optimizations.
To comply with this reasoning, the two approaches are
combined into a single distributed control architecture where
the capability of the DRL scheme to derive routing decisions
by mitigating the undesired traffic effects allows to drive safely
the vehicles until the assigned target without any occurrence

Then, the vehicle routing problem can be straightforwardly

There, the idea is to define a connected decision graph (the
upper graph of Figure 3) which describes the URN as follows:
a node accounts for a junction, while an edge represents the

procedure can be summarized as follows: associate to each
edge a positive length weight, compute all the paths connecting
given initial and final nodes, select the N paths shortest paths
computed in terms of the sum of the length weights. This
modus operandi has two benefits: mitigate the computational
complexity pertaining to the search procedure; guarantee the
existence of admissible path planning solutions, see lower
graph of Figure 3.

A. Computational Complexity Analysis
In the sequel, computational burdens pertaining to the DQL,
DMPC procedures and DRL-MPC algorithm are outlined and
summarized in Table I.
1) DQL: Let n 6 and n A be the maximum number of steps
per episode (bounded by the number of the nodes in the
URN) and the size of the action set A, respectively. Then,
in the off-line phase the DQL procedure has a computational
load polynomial in time O(n 2A ∗ n 6 ) while the on-line phase
involves O(n 2A ), see [51] and [52].
2) DMPC: In the offline phase, the computational complexity is related to the computation of the overlapped PI sets
via the two optimization problems DMPC-P L and DMPCP Fi . Both the optimizations are SDP and the computational
burdens are O(n 3 ). Conversely the on-line phase reduces to
the solution of the tracking one-step problem that is Quadratic
Programming (QP).
3) DRL-MPC Algorithm: In the off-line phase, the DMPCP L and DMPC-P Fi , i = 2, . . . , L , are invoked and the
DRL training. Conversely, on-line just a single iteration is
considered to update the reward function and the solution of
a QP optimization (32)-(33).

VII. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, the sustainable routing decision problem for
vehicle platoons operating in urban road networks has been
addressed. By jointly exploiting Deep Reinforcement Learning
and set-theoretic model predictive control arguments, a novel
distributed machine learning-control architecture has been
conceived and its main properties outlined. In the simulation
section, a platoon of five vehicles, operating in the city center
of Hamburg in Germany, has been used to enlight the main
features of the DRL-MPC algorithm. Simulations testify that
the proposed algorithm:
• reduces C O2 emissions and traffic congestion;
• avoids to increase the pollution of areas subject to
C O2 emission constraints.
Future studies will follow two directions. First, it is important
to extend the proposed approach to comply with obstacle
avoidance requirements and more roadway lanes. Then,
the DRL algorithm computational performance should be
increased in order to get faster routing decisions.

Giuseppe Franzè (Senior Member, IEEE) received
the Ph.D. degree in systems engineering from the
University of Calabria, Italy, in 1999. Since 2022,
he has been a Full Professor with the DIMEG
Department, University of Calabria. He has authored
or coauthored of more than 200 research papers in
archival journals, book chapters, and international
conference proceedings. His current research interests include constrained predictive control, nonlinear
systems, networked control systems, control under
constraints and control reconfiguration for fault
tolerant systems, and resilient control for cyber-physical systems. From
November 2019 to December 2019, he was a Visiting Professor with the CIISE
Department, Concordia University (CA). He currently serves as an Associate
Editor for the IEEE/CAA J OURNAL OF AUTOMATICA S INICA. He was the
Guest Editor of the Special Issue “Resilient Control in Large-Scale Networked
Cyber-Physical Systems” of IEEE/CAA J OURNAL OF AUTOMATICA S INICA
in 2020. From 2018 to 2022, he was the Graduate Program Director of
the master’s degree in robotics and automation engineering with the DIMES
Department, University of Calabria.

Francesco Pupo (Member, IEEE) received the
master’s degree (cum laude) in computer engineering
and the Ph.D. degree in systems and computer
engineering. He is currently an Associate Professor
in computer engineering with the Department of
Informatics, Modeling, Electronics, and Systems,
University of Calabria (UNICAL), Italy. He teaches
real time and distributed programming for the
MD in robotics and automation engineering, and
mobile devices for the MD in telecommunication
engineering. His research interests include real-time
systems, formal modeling and simulation, petri nets, intelligent agents,
machine learning, the Internet of Things, and cyber-physical systems. He has
authored several papers and regularly serves as a referee for journals and
conferences proceedings.

Giancarlo Fortino (Fellow, IEEE) received the
