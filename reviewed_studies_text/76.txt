Abstract— The increasing complexity of intelligent
transportation systems driven by the deployment of
connected autonomous vehicles necessitates advanced,
adaptive traffic management solutions. This paper
presents a novel hybrid learning architecture that
integrates deep reinforcement learning with ant colony
optimization to enable edge-aware, real-time routing
decisions for connected vehicle networks. Each vehicle
functions as an intelligent agent trained via a multi-agent
actor-critic deep reinforcement learning model, enhanced
with attention mechanisms for context-sensitive
navigation. Ant colony optimization is used to refine
global routing paths through pheromone-inspired
collective learning, ensuring adaptive optimization under
dynamic traffic conditions. The proposed approach is
formalized as ADAPT-S (Ant-Driven Adaptive Policy
Training with Simulation-Based Validation), combining
swarm intelligence, policy learning, and SUMO-based
simulation.
The
model
demonstrates
superior
performance in Simulation of Urban Mobility (SUMO)
environments, outperforming traditional routing
methods in reducing travel time, congestion, and signal
wait periods. The proposed method results validated the
framework’s effectiveness in addressing key limitations of
existing methods, particularly in achieving decentralized
coordination, scalability, and responsiveness in connected
autonomous vehicle routing systems.
Keywords— Connected Autonomous Vehicles (CAV), Deep
Reinforcement Learning (DRL), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO),
multi-agent actor-critic model, attention mechanism, Simulation
of Urban Mobility (SUMO), ADAPT-S Algorithm.

To achieve real-time decision-making in complex and
dynamic environments, advanced algorithmic models are
being deployed across ITS and CAV domains. Among them,
Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has shown remarkable
success in enabling adaptive traffic signal control, vehicle
routing, and behavioral prediction. DRL, particularly in
multi-agent configurations (MA-RL), empowers individual
vehicles or intersections to learn optimal actions based on
environmental feedback. Meanwhile, swarm intelligence
techniques like Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) are
employed for global route optimization due to their ability to
solve combinatorial problems through distributed,
pheromone-inspired search mechanisms. Recent hybrid
models have explored combining these paradigms to integrate
local adaptivity (via DRL) with global coordination (via
ACO), forming robust decision systems for high-density
traffic environments [2].

(ITS) and Connected Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs) has
accelerated over the past decade, focusing on real-time traffic
optimization, safety, and sustainability. Works such as
Autonomous Vehicles and Urban Traffic Management for

improvements in flow reliability by up to 25% but also

Systematic reviews underscore persistent issues including
latency, scalability, and dependence on centralized
infrastructure [4].
Algorithmic approaches for ITS/CAV management have
evolved from rule‑based and model‑predictive control to
machine learning and hybrid methods. Deep Reinforcement
Learning (DRL) models—such as multi-agent actor-critic
systems—have been employed for signal coordination and
platoon control, exhibiting strong adaptability in simulation
environments like SUMO [5]. However, purely DRL systems
struggle with convergence in dense traffic and often neglect
global optimization, while techniques like V2X-based
adaptive signal systems still rely on fixed detector inputs [6].

RESEARCH WORK PROPOSED

The increasing complexity of intelligent transportation
systems (ITS) and the growing deployment of connected
autonomous vehicles (CAVs) necessitate adaptive,
decentralized routing frameworks. To address limitations in
traditional centralized systems and standalone algorithms,
this research proposes a novel hybrid architecture—ADAPTS (Ant-Driven Adaptive Policy Training with SimulationBased Validation)—which combines the strengths of deep
reinforcement learning (DRL), swarm intelligence (ACO),
and attention mechanisms, validated in a simulated
environment using SUMO. This section outlines the core
design, algorithmic structure, mathematical formulation, and
optimization strategy of the proposed system.

A. System Architecture
The system architecture is designed to enable real-time
sensing, decision-making, and communication among
connected autonomous agents. Each vehicle in the network
acts as an independent decision-making node embedded with
a DRL agent, capable of learning optimal navigation policies
through interaction with its environment. The global
coordination of vehicle routes is achieved through an Ant
Colony Optimization mechanism that iteratively updates
shared routing preferences based on network-wide traffic
conditions. The simulation environment—built using
SUMO—replicates real-world urban traffic dynamics to
evaluate the performance of the framework under varied
congestion scenarios. Key components of the system include
multi-agent vehicle models, edge-level communication, and
adaptive route prioritization based on feedback and
environmental perception.
B. ADAPT-S Algorithm
The The ADAPT-S algorithm is designed to combine
local policy learning with global path optimization in a fully
decentralized manner. Each vehicle runs a multi-agent actorcritic DRL model, using attention mechanisms to prioritize
relevant features such as neighboring vehicle behaviors,
signal states, and road occupancy. Simultaneously, the Ant
Colony Optimization component operates at the network
level, emulating pheromone trails that guide agents toward
efficient paths based on cumulative learning from successful
trajectories. The integration of ACO with DRL enables agents
not only to adapt to local conditions but also to benefit from
globally optimized strategies. This
proposed model
illustrated in Figure 4, which is a hybrid approach enhances
convergence stability, responsiveness, and route diversity in
dense and dynamic traffic environments.

The global optimization is modeled through pheromone
update rules in Ant Colony Optimization (ACO):
τ_ij(t + 1) = (1 - ρ) τ_ij(t) + Δτ_ij
(2)
where ρ is the pheromone evaporation rate, and Δτ_ij is the
pheromone deposit proportional to the quality of the path.
This dual-model ensures both real-time policy refinement and
convergence to globally efficient traffic patterns.
D. Optimization Model
The optimization objective of the proposed ADAPT-S
framework is to minimize the total delay and congestion in
the CAV network while ensuring scalability and
decentralized control. The cost function is expressed as:
min ∑_i (α T_i + β ∑_j D_ij)

(3)

where T_i is the travel time for vehicle i, and D_ij is the
dynamic density on the link between nodes i and j. The
constants α and β are weight coefficients representing the
trade-off between delay minimization and traffic balancing.
The ACO mechanism evaluates multiple path combinations
by simulating virtual pheromone trails, favoring routes with
lower cumulative costs. Concurrently, the DRL agent refines
its policy through localized exploration and exploitation,
guided by reward feedback. The integration of these two
layers ensures real-time adaptivity while avoiding premature
convergence—a common issue in traditional DRL or
heuristic-based systems. As a result, ADAPT-S achieves
improved traffic distribution, reduced average travel times,
and efficient rerouting in response to dynamic road
conditions.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS

The proposed ADAPT-S framework was implemented and
evaluated in a simulated urban traffic environment using
SUMO, interfaced with a custom DRL-ACO module.
Evaluations focused on travel time, congestion levels, route
optimality, and system scalability under varying traffic
densities. Comparative analysis demonstrates ADAPT-S’s
performance advantages over baseline DRL and ACO-only
methods.

A. SUMO Simulation Model
In the SUMO-based testbed, a mid-sized urban network
comprising 20 intersections and 200 connected autonomous
vehicles (CAVs) was modeled. The hybrid ADAPT-S
algorithm yielded an average travel time reduction of 15.2%,
congestion decrease by 18.5%, and improved route
distribution compared to standard DRL and ACO
implementations. These results confirm ADAPT-S’s
capability to adapt dynamically to traffic fluctuations and
distribute load effectively across the network.

This study presented ADAPT-S, a hybrid learning
framework that integrates attention-guided multi-agent deep
reinforcement learning with ant colony optimization for
decentralized traffic management in connected autonomous
vehicle (CAV) networks. Through SUMO-based simulation
and comparative analysis, ADAPT-S demonstrated superior
performance in reducing average travel time, congestion, fuel
consumption, and rerouting frequency while achieving faster
policy convergence. The results validate the effectiveness of
combining local learning and global coordination in
achieving scalable, adaptive, and energy-efficient traffic
control. Future work will explore real-time deployment on
edge-based vehicular hardware, integration with V2X
communication standards, and expansion to multi-city smart
transportation networks.
