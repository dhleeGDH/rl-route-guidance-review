ABSTRACT With the rapid urbanization and increasing demand for transportation, cities are facing major

problems in mitigating traffic congestion and environmental pollution. A series of work have been done to
resolve these issues, yet great challenges still exist. Although multi-vehicle path planning is beneficial for
finding global optimal route, it significantly complicates the computation and places higher demands on
processing capabilities and response time. Meanwhile, existing congestion control methods mainly rely on
real-time traffic data to identify the occurrence of congestion, and take damage control actions afterwards.
This passive mode limits the effectiveness of these methods and introduce extra delay into the system.
In view of this, we propose a novel Heterogeneous-agent rEinforcement Route Optimization (HERO)
framework that combines fog and cloud computing to optimize traffic management. The system integrates
global traffic data analysis in the cloud with real-time local traffic monitoring and adjustment through fog
nodes, where cloud and roadside units (RSUs) collaborate to efficiently manage multi-vehicle path planning
and congestion control. An Attention-CNN-LSTM-based prediction model is designed for surveying
congestion tendency in each road segment, which provides basis for RSUs to take proactive response for
avoiding congestion. The system employs a heterogeneous-agent advantage actor-critic (HAA2C) network
to capture collaboration and interactions among heterogeneous agents, optimizing the global strategy while
accommodating agent diversity. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed architecture exhibits
satisfying scalability and performance in reducing energy consumption, pollution emissions, and enhancing
traffic efficiency through successful congestion avoidance and traffic flow balance.

I. INTRODUCTION

The pervasive adoption of automobiles has greatly improved
mobility but has also intensified traffic congestion and environmental pollution [1], [2]. Congestion forces vehicles
to travel at low speeds for extended periods, resulting in
energy waste and excessive emissions. To address these
issues, intelligent transportation systems (ITS) [3] integrate
communication, sensing, and control technologies to enhance
network efficiency. As a core component of ITS, the route
guidance system (RGS) leverages congestion perception and
route planning to analyze real-time traffic data and provide
optimal routing suggestions for vehicles.

However, traditional RGS faces several limitations. First,
its vehicle-oriented routing strategies lack a global perspective, often causing multiple vehicles to select the same “optimal” route, inadvertently creating new congestion hotspots.
Second, many RGS solutions rely on single-objective optimization centered on travel time, neglecting fuel efficiency
and carbon emissions [4], [5]. As a result, routing decisions
may reduce travel time but worsen overall environmental
impact.
Recent research attempts to overcome these limitations
through global optimization, real-time traffic monitoring, and
multi-objective path planning [6], [7], [8]. Global optimization techniques [9] consider both current and predicted [10]

weighted optimization to jointly minimize travel time, fuel
consumption, and emissions [16]. These developments enable modern RGS to operate more efficiently in dynamic
traffic environments.
Nevertheless, multi-vehicle routing on large-scale road
networks substantially increases computational complexity
and reduces responsiveness. Moreover, most existing systems
rely primarily on real-time data and lack the capability to
predict future congestion, resulting in passive responses that
occur only after congestion forms. This reactive nature limits
their effectiveness in fast-changing traffic conditions.
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a multiobjective RGS that integrates heterogeneous-agent reinforcement learning (HARL) [17] within a fog–cloud architecture. The system combines global routing performed by the
cloud with real-time congestion management by RSU agents,
achieving proactive and coordinated traffic optimization. The
main contributions are as follows:
1) We integrate the ITS fog–cloud architecture with a
heterogeneous multi-agent RL framework to jointly
optimize global and local routing decisions. The cloud
agent performs long-horizon global path planning,
while fog-based RSU agents execute real-time local
adjustments based on traffic feedback. This cooperation improves scalability, responsiveness, and robustness in dynamic, large-scale networks.
2) We convert traditional per-vehicle rerouting into flowlevel decision-making by using the rerouting ratio as
each RSU agent’s action. This significantly reduces
action dimensionality, computation cost, and communication overhead. RSU agents dynamically adjust
rerouting ratios to mitigate local congestion, while the
cloud agent ensures system-wide coordination.
3) We introduce the concept of congestion tendency,
combining attention mechanisms, CNN, and LSTM
models to predict future traffic flow variations. This
enables proactive rerouting before congestion forms,
enhancing path assignment accuracy and contributing
to sustainable traffic management.

II. RELATED WORKS

The core tasks of RGS include congestion perception and
congestion avoidance, which are closely interconnected and
work together to achieve efficient traffic management.

In [18], it is shown that the shortest path in real road
networks often passes through highly congested, low-speed
segments with frequent acceleration and deceleration, leading to higher energy consumption and emissions than longer
but smoother routes. As a result, the shortest path is neither the most energy-efficient nor necessarily the fastest,
highlighting that effective congestion awareness is essential
for reducing emissions and improving traffic efficiency. To
achieve efficient congestion perception, the study in [19]
proposes a comprehensive traffic congestion index (TCI)
based on speed, density, and acceleration. By dynamically
monitoring speed drops, density increases, and acceleration
anomalies, it identifies congested road sections and leverages
cloud platforms to optimize detour costs. In [7], real-time
data such as road density, vehicle speed, and road capacity
are introduced. A weighted formula is used to enhance
the accuracy of congestion assessment, and a deterministic
weight model is constructed to optimize route allocation
while balancing traffic efficiency and carbon emissions.
In [20], a collaborative vehicle rerouting system is designed,
which uses road density combined with a support vector
regression (SVR) model to predict future traffic conditions,
dynamically adjust vehicle arrival times, and update road
capacity, thus improving the coordination and timeliness of
path planning.
In terms of congestion avoidance, [21] proposes a k -order
routing strategy based on Copula scenario generation and
a two-stage algorithm to enhance lane-changing decisions
and travel efficiency. In [22], an event-based path service
management system is developed, which avoids secondary
congestion by sensing remaining road capacity and vehicle speed. In [23], the OppIN system is proposed for
emergency response path intervention by integrating IoT
infrastructure, Big Data technologies, and multi-criteria path
evaluation. This work demonstrates that dynamic traffic conditions, road status, and context-aware information should
be jointly considered for quasi-real-time route planning
in critical transportation scenarios. Similarly, Armayau et
al. [24] investigated dynamic vehicle routing optimization
for urban distribution under real-time demand fluctuations,
where short-term demand prediction and dynamic information processing are combined to improve routing efficiency
under uncertain urban conditions. In [25], a spatiotemporal
routing method based on volume-to-capacity (v/c) ratio is
proposed by combining historical and real-time data, effectively bypassing heavily loaded road sections. In [26],
control transitions in automated vehicles are modeled and a
TD3-based strategy is used to optimize take-over requests,
effectively mitigating ToC-induced congestion and improving traffic efficiency. The study in [27] constructs a path
flexibility index to evaluate potential congestion risks and
enhance the robustness of the traffic network. In [28], model
predictive control (MPC) and deep reinforcement learning
(DRL) are integrated to propose a regional route guidance
framework, which employs multi-level strategies and reward

However, congestion perception and avoidance conducted
solely on local segments cannot effectively resolve the
cascading effects of traffic congestion. Chen et al. [29]
modeled congestion propagation in urban rail transit networks using an epidemic-inspired SAIS model and showed
that awareness-based preventive responses can suppress the
spread of congestion. Although their study focuses on
passenger-flow congestion in rail transit systems, it further
indicates that congestion may propagate through network
topology and should be addressed from a network-level
perspective. To this end, researchers have proposed the
concept of global route guidance (GRG). In [9], a centralized
flow-based guidance system is developed to evaluate detour
paths based on capacity and time loss, minimizing global
efficiency degradation.
Recent studies also show that coordination among multiple
traffic participants or control entities is essential for improving traffic efficiency. Vitale et al. [30] proposed a distributed
signal-free intersection optimization method for connected
and automated vehicles, where iterative time-slot adjustment
enables vehicles to coordinate intersection crossing with
limited information exchange. This demonstrates the value
of distributed coordination and low-communication decisionmaking, which is consistent with the motivation of introducing RSU-level local control in the proposed fog–cloud route
guidance framework. The ReFOCUS+ system in [16] integrates multi-layer path guidance and a dynamic road weight
measurement (RWM) model, coordinating local and global
route optimization. In [31], spatial features are extracted
using CNNs, vehicle interactions are modeled with graph
attention networks (GATs), and multi-agent rerouting is
achieved through vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) communication,
significantly improving path allocation efficiency. In [32],
a system optimal (SO) route guidance framework led by a
traffic management center (TMC) is proposed, combining
multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) and neural fictitious self-play (NFSP) to model user decision dynamics
and balance user preferences with system efficiency.
In addition, [33] proposes a hierarchical control framework, in which the upper layer predicts critical density
thresholds and dynamically adjusts routing weights, while
the lower layer generates energy-efficient trajectories for
connected and automated vehicles (CAVs). In [34], a twolevel optimization framework is built for both network-level
planning and vehicle-level trajectory control, incorporating
the Floyd algorithm and traffic signal constraints. In [35],
GAQ and EBkSP algorithms are integrated under a fog-cloud
architecture to achieve centralized learning and distributed
decision-making. The study in [36] implements hotspot identification and real-time traffic monitoring through VANET
and fuzzy logic.In [37], CNN is employed to predict travel
time and optimize two-route choices. The study in [38] establishes a bidirectional game mechanism between CAVs and

the information provider, enabling suggestion-driven path
coordination. Finally, [39] combines physics-based models
and reinforcement learning to construct the TransRL system,
in which a teacher policy guides learning to enhance the
stability and generalization of routing strategies under largescale and uncertain environments.
Existing studies on path planning span heuristic optimization, graph neural networks (GNNs), multi-agent coordination, and more recently, federated reinforcement learning.
For heuristic approaches, In [40], an improved ant colony
algorithm incorporating artificial potential fields, a refined
state transition strategy, and multi-objective pheromone updating is proposed to achieve faster, safer, and smoother
global path planning for ships in both static and dynamic
environments. In the field of cooperative path planning,
Huang et al. [41] developed a time-dependent cooperative
path-planning model for shipborne multi-UAV systems in
port atmospheric pollution monitoring. Their deep learning–
dynamic ant colony optimization method adaptively tunes
key ACO parameters and improves convergence under dynamic spatiotemporal constraints. This study indicates that
hybrid learning-based heuristic optimization can enhance
path-planning performance in dynamic and multi-agent environments. [42] develops an improved particle swarm optimizer (DSI-PSO) that dynamically adjusts an S-shaped
inertia weight based on the particle–global-best distance,
accelerating convergence and avoiding local minima, thereby
achieving short, smooth, and collision-free single-vehicle
paths.
Learning-based methods have also progressed rapidly. [43]
models VRPTPLR as a fully connected graph and adopts
an NNConv-GNN encoder to explicitly capture reward–cost
relationships between nodes, yielding 0.24%–0.49% performance gains over PNN/LSTM at the cost of significantly
higher training complexity. Huang et al. [44] proposed
DTLight, a lightweight Decision Transformer-based offlineto-online RL method for traffic signal control. By combining offline datasets, adaptive knowledge distillation, and
online fine-tuning, DTLight improves sample efficiency and
reduces computational cost for practical deployment. This
work reflects the recent trend of integrating sequence modeling and reinforcement learning to address dynamic traffic
control problems. Authors in [45] embeds task graphs via
GNNs, predicts congestion using XGBoost, and integrates
green parameters into a weighted graph on which Dijkstra
search is performed for multi-depot routing. [46] proposes a
distributed multi-agent planner combining CNN and GNN:
CNNs extract local features, while GNNs propagate neighborhood information to enable multi-hop perception and
cooperative collision avoidance even under limited observations. [47] employs a context-aware GNN with attention
to dynamically model nodes and edges for joint route and
charging-station selection, enhanced by SHAP and LIME
for interpretability. [48] formulates routing paths and link
resources as a bipartite graph and uses GNN message passing

In summary, existing studies have made progress in congestion perception and avoidance, but challenges remain in
improving the coordination between global and local optimization, computational efficiency, and system scalability.
For one thing, traditional vehicle-by-vehicle path adjustments are computationally intensive, and the communication
delay further hinders efficiency. For another, the existing
congestion prediction methods often rely on historical data
and real-time conditions, which lacks foresight for proactive intervention. Moreover, the system’s responsiveness and
scalability in large-scale dynamic scenarios remain inadequate, limiting its ability to address complex traffic demands effectively.Despite these advances, notable limitations
remain. GNNs struggle with long-range dependency modeling, dynamic traffic adaptation, and scalability in large
road networks; heuristic methods suffer exponential complexity growth and local-optimality issues under large-scale
dynamic constraints; independent-agent RL models cause
exploding state spaces and communication overheads in
highly dynamic urban networks; and although FRL reduces
communication and preserves privacy, non-IID distributions
and vehicle heterogeneity lead to policy inconsistency, while
gradient leakage still poses privacy risks.

III. SYSTEM MODEL AND PROBLEM FORMULATION
A. System Model

The system model of the considered HARL-based RGS is
shown in Fig. 1. The system adopts a fog-cloud collaborative
architecture. Vehicles traveling in the road network constitute
the bottom layer, referred to as the vehicle layer. RSUs
are deployed at major signalized intersections and form the
middle layer, namely the fog layer. The top layer is the cloud
layer, which consists of cloud servers. It is assumed that the
communication between the fog layer and the cloud layer is
supported by a reliable wired backbone network to ensure
stable and timely transmission of global traffic information
and control decisions. Communication between vehicles and
RSUs is enabled through V2X links, which support vehicle
status reporting, route requests, and rerouting instruction
delivery.
The traffic network can be modeled as a graph G(A, E),
where A = {a1 , a2 , . . . , ak , . . .} denotes the set of vehicles,
and E = {e11 , e12 , . . . , eij , . . .} represents the set of road
segments. Here, eij denotes the road segment connecting
the i-th and the j -th intersections, and ak represents the k -th
vehicle.

In each time slot, when a vehicle ak is about to enter
the traffic network, it sends a message containing its realtime status information to the associated RSU within its
communication coverage via V2X communication, requesting route planning service. Upon receiving the request, the
RSU uploads the vehicle information together with realtime road data collected by multiple onboard sensors to the
cloud server through the backbone communication network.
After aggregating information from the fog layer, the cloud
layer obtains a global view of the traffic network and is
capable of generating an optimal path planning strategy for
the requesting vehicle. The computed route is then delivered
to the corresponding RSU and subsequently forwarded to the
vehicle.
However, due to the highly dynamic nature of traffic conditions, the initially assigned optimal path may not remain
optimal throughout the entire trip. Therefore, RSUs deployed
at intersections are responsible for congestion perception on
their covered road segments. When an RSU detects a potential congestion risk, it learns to make rerouting decisions,
indicating which vehicles on the affected segment should be
rerouted and how. The selected vehicles then update their
routes based on the latest global traffic information obtained
from the cloud layer and leave the congested segment in
advance, thereby mitigating congestion formation.

IV. HETEROGENEOUS-AGENT REINFORCEMENT
LEARNING-BASED ROUTE OPTIMIZATION

To solve the above problem, this study proposes a green and
efficient mechanism that integrates fog and cloud computing, combining with a HAA2C-based algorithm to achieve
traffic flow balancing and congestion avoidance in complex
transportation networks. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the proposed
HERO mechanism employs a global optimization model on
the cloud to characterize the real-time traffic conditions on
road segments, and realizes efficient and fast path allocation
based on that. In the meantime, RSUs deploy an AttentionCNN-LSTM module to predict the next time-step traffic flow
on specific road segments, identifying congestion trends and
estimating congestion probabilities. This predictive capability enables proactive decision-making to mitigate congestion
on RSUs. A HAA2C-based algorithm is then applied for
global and local collaborative optimization, using traffic network information as the environment input for heterogeneous
multi-agent reinforcement learning, effectively enhancing the
system’s performance in real-time route planning and traffic
management.

C. HAA2C-based Routing Optimization

In this study, we propose a HAA2C-based routing optimization algorithm for avoiding congestion and enhancing traffic
efficiency, as well as reducing pollution emissions. HAA2C
network leverages the joint advantage function to capture
collaboration and interactions among heterogeneous agents,
optimizing the global strategy while accommodating agent
diversity. Unlike traditional multi-agent algorithms such as
Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO), which
assume agent homogeneity and rely on shared policies,
HAA2C allows agents to independently adjust their strategies based on joint states and actions, enabling effective
handling of diverse roles and objectives in heterogeneous
systems.
As shown in Fig. 3, the training of HAA2C consists of two
parts. In the first stage, agents interact with the environment
to collect trajectory data. Each trajectory contains the global
state st , local observations of each agent oit , actions ait ,
immediate rewards rt , and the next state st+1 as well as the
next local observations oit+1 . These data are stored in a replay buffer. In the second stage, the joint advantage function
Aim
π (sb , aim ) is computed to guide the policy updates, and
the parameters of each agent’s policy network are adjusted
using policy gradient methods. During the process, the order
of agents is randomly shuffled, and the policy networks of
the agents are updated sequentially. Meanwhile, the critic
network optimizes the state value function V (s) based on the
joint states and actions by minimizing the value estimation
error, thereby improving network performance.
In the considered system, there involves two types of
agents, the cloud agent and the RSU agent. Cloud agent first
collects global traffic information, including vehicle number
on all road segments, as well as the current planned vehicle
routes and the distribution ratio for each route. These data
provide the basis for global optimization. RSU agent collects
local traffic information for its associated road segments,
including vehicle number and congestion tendency, ensuring that local traffic issues can be quickly identified and
adjusted. After collecting the relevant information, cloud
agent performs route assignment actions based on the global

V. PERFORMANCE EVALUATION

To evaluate the proposed HERO algorithm, we conduct
a comprehensive performance comparison against several
baseline and enhanced methods. Initially, the evaluation
is carried out on a medium-scale Chicago road network
consisting of 107 nodes, where HERO is compared with
the following three algorithms: 1) ReFOCUS+ [16], a multilayer advanced road guidance system based on a joint fogcloud system. ReFOCUS+ takes into account the effects of
regional congestion, road congestion, and potential future
traffic conditions for congestion detection. 2) DSP [57],
whenever congestion is detected on a road segment, it
collects the set of vehicles whose paths intersect with that
segment and calculates a new shortest path for each vehicle
based on the current travel times of the road network. 3)
RSU-only, an algorithm proposed in this study that removes
the cloud component and focuses on local optimization by
RSUs, relying solely on RSUs for local traffic management.
To further validate the scalability and robustness of HERO,
we extend the evaluation to a larger Chicago network with
149 nodes and include additional competitive algorithms
for comparison: 4) CVRS [20], a congestion-aware vehicle
rerouting strategy based on predictive information. CVRS
dynamically selects rerouting vehicles by considering travel
time and updates the remaining road capacity in real-time
to enable cooperative rerouting among vehicles. 5) MAPPO,
where the HERO system is optimized using the Multi-Agent
Proximal Policy Optimization algorithm to assess its impact
on coordination under heterogeneous-agent settings.
A. Simulation Settings

In the simulation, we employ the Simulation of Urban Mobility (SUMO) version 1.20.0 as the traffic simulation platform
and construct a reinforcement learning training framework
based on the SUMO-RL environment. The experimental
environment is deployed on a desktop computer equipped
with an Intel Core i5-13500H six-core processor, 16 GB of
RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4050 Laptop GPU.
Model implementation and evaluation are conducted using
Python 3.8.19 and PyTorch 2.0.1, while the training and
inference processes are accelerated via CUDA 11.7 and
cuDNN 8.5.0.

The reason for Attention-CNN-LSTM’s significant performance advantage is primarily the unique architecture and
ability of it to handle complex spatio-temporal features.
For one thing, the Attention-CNN-LSTM model combines
the ability of the CNN layer to extract local features with
the LSTM’s strength in capturing long-term dependencies.
Moreover, the attention mechanism focuses on critical time
points. This design enables the model to effectively capture
complex temporal patterns in traffic flow, resulting in the
lowest prediction error (RMSE = 0.075815) and the highest
fit (R2 = 0.795068).

to the vanishing gradient problem, leading to a higher
prediction error (RMSE = 0.086323) and a lower fit (R2 =
0.734322). The CNN model, although able to extract features
through convolutional layers, lacks a dedicated mechanism
for modeling temporal dependencies, resulting in a higher
error (RMSE = 0.083664) and a lower fit (R2 = 0.750437).

VI. CONCLUSION

This study explores a congestion-avoidance and trafficbalancing framework within a fog–cloud architecture for
IoVs, aiming to enhance overall traffic system efficiency
through optimized path planning and real-time adaptive
management. We propose HERO, a reinforcement learning–based method that integrates the HAA2C framework
with traffic flow equilibrium to achieve proactive congestion
mitigation and dynamic adjustment. HERO forms a multiagent collaborative architecture in which the cloud performs
global traffic optimization and equilibrium analysis, while
RSUs monitor local traffic conditions and implement flowlevel adjustments in real time. This design enables the system
to intervene before congestion emerges, optimizing vehicle
allocation and routing strategies to prevent bottlenecks. The
adaptive nature of reinforcement learning allows HERO
to update decisions based on real-time traffic dynamics,
overcoming the limitations of static or shortest-path-based
traditional methods.
In addition, HERO employs an Attention–CNN–LSTM
model to predict short-term traffic evolution, enabling RSUs
to evaluate congestion trends and determine whether preemptive actions are required. The flow-equilibrium optimization
model further allows agents to learn re-routing strategies
under diverse traffic conditions and dynamically adjust the
proportion of vehicles to be rerouted.
Simulation results demonstrate that HERO significantly
outperforms traditional approaches such as DSP and ReFOCUS+ in terms of travel time, energy consumption, and
carbon dioxide emissions. By proactively predicting congestion and optimizing routing strategies, the system effectively
reduces congestion in high-density areas and minimizes idle
and low-speed driving, leading to reduced energy usage and
emissions.
Despite its strong performance, several directions remain
open for further extension of the framework. First, the
current method primarily focuses on flow-level coordination,
and finer-grained individual vehicle requirements are not yet
fully modeled; the interaction between global optimization
and autonomous vehicle-level decision-making may also become more diverse under complex traffic conditions. Second,
